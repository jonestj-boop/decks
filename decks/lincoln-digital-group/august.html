Why the 90-day total still looks positive

A 90-day rolling total can rise even while every recent month falls — it's still being compared against Jan–Apr, a period with much lower baseline traffic. That's a real number, but it masks the month-over-month slide shown at left.
It isn't the striking-distance keywords. The 5 highest-intent opportunity queries flagged in May — the ones closest to page one, geo-anchored, buyer-ready — have moved in the wrong direction on 3 of 5 terms. See next slide.
It isn't new content. No evidence in Search Console of the 120-article production plan going live. The same handful of legacy blog posts are still doing all the work — and two of them are actively losing rankings.

What else GA4 shows since April

Direct traffic is down too — 736 sessions in April to 655 in July — ruling out "it's just organic" as an explanation. The overall site is cooling, not just search.
Paid Social and Display traffic appeared starting June (388 Display + 142 Paid Social sessions in June; 371 Display + 41 Paid Social in July) — a paid campaign is now propping up total traffic while organic keeps sliding underneath it.
A new "AI Assistant" channel appeared in June (26 sessions, growing to 38 in July) — a small but emerging source worth watching as AI-driven search referrals grow industry-wide.

What this means

The site lost its best beachhead. In May, Google AI Overview was the strongest signal — 3 mentions on 7 cited pages, proof the engine already trusted the brand enough to name it. That's now zero.
ChatGPT and Gemini picked up small, likely incidental gains (0→2 and 0→1 mentions) — not the result of deliberate work, since nothing from the roadmap's plan has been actioned.
Cited pages fell from 49 to 9 — an 82% drop. AI engines are reading far fewer pages as sources than in May. This tracks exactly with the traditional-search decline: the same content is losing visibility everywhere, not just in Google rankings.
