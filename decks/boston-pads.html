Phase 2 — Own

How A Winning Keyword Becomes A Content Cluster

A proven keyword doesn't get one blog post. It gets a cluster — a hub page, supporting articles, internal links and schema — engineered to own the whole intent neighborhood around it.

Worked example

Assume the landlord cell returns the strongest economics in the test.

Paid signal rent out my apartment cambridge produces owner signups at a cost well under their value
Hub page /cambridge-ma-landlords/ — how to list, what Cambridge rents actually are, your lease-up process
Cluster 8–12 supporting articles: Cambridge rent trends, screening tenants in MA, Sept 1 turnover playbook, broker fee law, vacancy cost math
Local SEO LocalBusiness + FAQ schema, GBP posts targeting owners, internal links from the 105 renter hubs
Scale Template proven in Cambridge, then rolled to Somerville, Brookline, Medford, Malden — the markets the test ranked highest

Why clusters, not one-off posts

  • You already know this works. Your 990 modifier subpages are a cluster strategy — we're applying the same logic to the revenue side of the business
  • Topical authority compounds. A single landlord page competes alone; 12 interlinked pages on Cambridge landlording signal genuine subject depth
  • It feeds AI answers. Entity-rich, well-structured clusters are what answer engines actually retrieve and cite
  • The paid budget retires. As clusters rank, spend shifts off those keywords and onto the next test cell — the engine keeps funding its own expansion
The compounding effect: every month the test runs, the content plan gets sharper and the paid budget gets more efficient. You stop paying for traffic you've learned to earn.

You Opened The Door To AI. You're Just Not In The Answers Yet.

This is genuinely unusual: your technical AI readiness is ahead of almost every local business we audit. The infrastructure is done. The citations are missing.

Somebody on your side did this deliberately. That's a real head start — you've made yourself maximally retrievable to exactly the engines that matter, and closed the door on the ones that just resell your data.

What's missing

  • Crawlability isn't citation. Being allowed to be read is necessary, not sufficient. AI answers cite sources that are discussed, not just indexed
  • Renters ask communities, not portals. "Is Allston worth it?", "best neighborhood for a BU grad student", "is a broker fee normal in Boston" — these get asked on Reddit, and that's disproportionately what answer engines retrieve
  • Your best asset is unsyndicated. You publish original Boston rental market data monthly. That's exactly the kind of citable, first-party statistic AI answers reach for — and it currently lives only on your blog
  • No presence in the discussion layer. r/boston, r/bostonhousing and the university subreddits are where the Sept 1 scramble plays out every year
The fit: AEO placements put your market data into the conversations renters and landlords are already having, in the venues answer engines cite most. It runs independently of the SEO engagement — pricing on the next slide.
