Across April–August, organic clicks are down 37% year over year and have declined every month of 2026 — from 342 in April to 117 in August. The practice's branded demand is steady; the loss is entirely in the informational blog content that drove last summer's traffic.

What's holding, what's slipping

  • Holding — branded search ("locklear dentistry," "dr. locklear") is essentially flat YoY at ~270 clicks. People looking for the practice by name still find it at the top.
  • Slipping — non-branded informational traffic (the "foods after a tooth extraction" post and the "soda after a filling" cluster) has lost roughly half its clicks. That content carried 2025's growth and is now receding.
  • Misleading bright spot — average position "improved" from 38.2 to 22.5, but that's a side effect of losing a large volume of deep-ranked impressions, not better rankings on terms that convert (see Visibility).

Impressions, CTR & the position paradox

Visibility — how often and how high the site surfaces in results — is the leading indicator behind the click decline. Impressions are down 42% YoY, and the "improved" average position needs context.

The position paradox: the Apr–Aug average position looks better YoY (22.5 vs 38.2), but that's because the site lost tens of thousands of deep-ranked impressions (page 4+ appearances that never earned clicks). Fewer low-ranking impressions pulls the average up. Within 2026 itself, position actually worsened month over month — 15.5 in April to 38.1 in August — which lines up with the falling clicks.
  • CTR is fine — at 0.84% it's slightly better than last year. The problem isn't that people aren't clicking; it's that the site is being shown for fewer searches.
  • Impressions lead clicks — the June impressions collapse (−39% MoM) preceded the summer's steepest click losses. Visibility is contracting first.
  • Mobile is the majority — 64% of 2026 clicks (657 of 1,029) come from mobile, consistent with 2025's mix.

Interpretation

What this means

  • The practice's core is safe. Branded, name-driven search is steady and ranks at the top. Reputation and direct demand are healthy — this is not a brand problem.
  • The blog engine is running out of fuel. The informational content that drove 2025's summer peak is aging and losing impression volume. Without fresh or refreshed content, the decline continues.
  • Local, high-intent terms are the untapped lane. "dentist greensboro nc" and similar commercial-intent searches generate impressions but sit too low to convert. That's where new patients are, and where the site is currently absent.
  • Watch impressions, not the position average. The position number flatters the trend. Impressions and clicks both tell the truth: visibility is contracting, and it has been all year.
Bottom line: steady brand, fading content, and a wide-open local opportunity. Reversing the trend means refreshing the informational content that's decaying and building for the Greensboro local terms the site doesn't yet rank for.
