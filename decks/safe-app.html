Content & authority · Red flags

Lots of content, little trust behind it.

You've published roughly 150 blog posts and 17 guides. The topics are right. What's missing is structure and authority: pages that overlap each other, and a link profile padded with spam that Google ignores.

What we saw on the blog

  • Several near-identical posts on the same day, such as multiple "hidden costs of Stripe chargebacks" articles. Pages like that compete with each other, and often none of them ranks.
  • Titles that still say "in 2024" on posts published in 2025 and 2026
  • Most posts dated Dec 2025 to Apr 2026, then publishing slows to a stop in July
  • No clear hub pages tying posts into topics (Stripe disputes, Shopify disputes, PayPal, friendly fraud)
Good news: this is a fix, not a rebuild. Merging the duplicates, refreshing the dates and adding hub pages gives the existing library a real chance to rank before a single new article goes live.

Who buys and who you're up against

The buyer searches right after a dispute lands, or right after one is lost.

Merchants rarely shop for chargeback help ahead of time. They search when a Stripe or Shopify dispute email shows up, when a fee hits, or when their dispute rate gets a warning. That moment is narrow, and it's where Safe has to show up.

The model · Rent to own

Low volume means every search has to earn its place.

Chargeback searches are small in number and high in value. We don't guess which ones to build content around. We rent each keyword with a fixed test budget, see whether it produces connected stores, and only then invest in owning it.

Rent · Month 1 onward

Test the keywords

  • Exact and phrase match on 20–30 merchant-intent searches
  • Each keyword gets a set test budget and a pass/fail line
  • Landing pages built for Shopify, Stripe and switchers
  • Meta retargeting for everyone who visits or reads a guide
→
Learn · Weekly

Keep what converts

  • Which searches lead to a connected Stripe or Shopify account
  • Which merchants are worth the most: dispute volume and order size
  • Which hook wins: 20% fee, $0 if you lose, or "we do the work"
  • Kill keywords that miss the line, move budget to winners
→
Own · Month 1 onward

Buy the keyword outright

  • Every winning keyword gets a ClusterMagic page built to rank for it
  • Brand mentions in the "best chargeback app" lists AI tools read
  • Top-of-funnel guides that fill the retargeting pool
  • As pages rank, bids on those terms come down
Why this works for a success-fee business: your revenue per merchant depends on how many disputes they win, not on a sign-up. Paid data tells us fast which searches bring merchants with real dispute volume. Content then takes over those searches, and you stop paying per click for buyers you already know convert.

Brand visibility in AI answers

When a merchant asks ChatGPT for a chargeback tool, Safe should be in the list.

More merchants now ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI Overview instead of clicking through ten results. Those tools build their answers from list articles, reviews and comparison pages. With a generic name and zero reviews, Safe gets left out today.

Prompts we'll track from week one

PromptWhat wins it

"What are chargeback prevention alerts?"Stopper explainer plus third-party mentions
We asked ChatGPT the first one on Nov 18. It recommended Chargeflow ("best overall"), Disputifier and Signifyd. Safe wasn't mentioned. We'll run the full set across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews before launch, then report monthly.

How we get Safe named

  • Brand mentions: placements in "best chargeback tools" and "how to handle Shopify disputes" style posts on established sites
  • Entity clarity: always "Safe App, chargeback recovery for Stripe and Shopify", never bare "Safe", so AI tools stop confusing you with safety apps
  • Comparison pages: your /compare pages, kept factual and current
  • Reviews: an ask to every merchant after their first win. Zero reviews is the easiest gap to close
  • llms.txt: you already have one. We keep it in sync with pricing and products
Fix the details AI tools repeat: today the site lists PayPal as available on one page and leaves it off others, the company name appears as both LLC and Inc, and Stopper is priced "per alert you get" in one place and "per alert handled" in another. AI answers pick up those mismatches. We'll flag each one for your team.
