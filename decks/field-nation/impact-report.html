Measurement issues

Five tracking problems to fix first

We found these five issues while pulling the data for this report. Each one lists what we saw, where we saw it, and how it affects the numbers.

01

Key events count far more than sign-ups and sales actions

What we found: GA4 has 22 key events. Five of them are ioj1_impression, ioj2_introduced, ioj3_interested, ioj3_investigated, and ioj5_initiated, all active (GA4 Admin, Key events).

What your GA4 exports show:

GA4 report (organic)Key eventsCompared withGA4 rate shown
Traffic acquisition, Sep 18, 2021 to Mar 31, 202475,646662,590 sessions4.0% of sessions had one
Traffic acquisition, Apr 1, 2024 to Oct 13, 20261,675,744619,954 sessions82.4% of sessions had one
User acquisition, Sep 18, 2021 to Mar 31, 202467,747263,249 users5.6% of users had one
User acquisition, Apr 1, 2024 to Oct 13, 20261,536,583242,370 users93.5% of users had one

Why we think these numbers don't reflect conversions:

  • Organic key events were 22 times higher in the second period while organic sessions fell 6%, and the share of organic users with a key event went from 5.6% to 93.5%.
  • On 116 of the 124 article URLs (the 101 articles' old /learn/ and new /resources/ addresses), GA4 records more key events than page views. The old /learn/ URL of MDF to IDF Cabling alone shows 703 key events against 315 page views.
  • The median article landing page has a 100% session key event rate, but sessions that started on an article led to only 6 sign-up and sales events (see Conversions).
  • In the Key event attribution models report for the last 6 months, organic search is credited with 282,281 key events when all 22 are counted, and 7,216 (2.6%) when only the 4 sign-up and sales events are counted.

Table figures are from the Traffic acquisition and User acquisition exports you sent; the article page view, key event and session figures in the reasons below are from your Pages and screens and Landing page exports (organic filters as listed in Sources); the 6 sign-up and sales events are from GA4 on Oct 14, 2026. The rates shown are GA4's own key event rates: the share of sessions or users with at least one key event, not key events divided by sessions. GA4 later showed 1,675,785 for the Traffic acquisition key events, as data for the last days finished processing.

Fix: confirm which events your team counts as conversions, then set key events to match. Events that aren't conversions can still be collected without key event status.

P1 · CriticalEffort: Low
02

47% of sessions have no landing page

What we found: in the GA4 Landing page report, 1,113,888 of 2,349,614 sessions from April 2024 to October 2026 have a landing page of "(not set)".

The effect: those sessions don't appear under any page in landing page reports, so they can't be counted toward any article or page. The article session counts in this report come from the other 53%. Page views come from a different report (Pages and screens).

Fix: find out why these sessions record no landing page (for example, sessions with no page view event) and fix the cause.

P1 · CriticalEffort: Medium
03

The Search Console data in GA4 is close to zero

What we found: GA4 is linked to Search Console. Its "Google organic search traffic" report shows 1 click and 260 impressions for all /resources/ pages combined, April 2024 to October 2026.

The effect: GA4 recorded 33,972 organic search sessions landing on /resources/ pages in the same period, and Semrush shows one of those pages ranking #3 for a keyword with 22,200 monthly searches. The linked data doesn't match either source, so it can't be used for clicks, impressions, or rankings. This report uses Semrush for rankings instead.

Fix: check which Search Console property is linked to GA4, link the domain property for fieldnation.com, and give EmberTribe read access.

P1 · HighEffort: Low
04

Technician sign-up counts are always even

What we found: in the GA4 Landing page report (April 2024 to October 2026), every provider_signup count we checked on /resources/ pages was an even number: 2, 4, 6, 10, 22, 56. None were odd.

The effect: if the event fires twice per sign-up, technician sign-ups in GA4 are double the real number. We haven't confirmed this, so this report shows provider_signup counts as GA4 reports them, with a note.

Fix: compare GA4's provider_signup count for a set period with the sign-ups recorded in your own system. If GA4 is double, fix the trigger.

P2 · MediumEffort: Low
05

Google still ranks the old /learn/ URLs

What we found: all 101 articles now live at /resources/, and their old /learn/ URLs redirect there (we loaded each one). In Semrush, 364 of the articles' 434 ranking keywords (84%) still list the old /learn/ URL.

The effect: every visitor from those results goes through a redirect before the article loads. In GA4, the same article's visits are split across a /learn/ row and a /resources/ row, so they have to be added together to see an article's full traffic, as we did for this report.

Fix: update internal links and the XML sitemap to the /resources/ URLs, and confirm the redirects return a 301 (permanent) status.
