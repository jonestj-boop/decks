The opportunity

Raise The Bar Therapy already has what every new coffee cart needs most: a steady stream of people walking through the door, week after week, at predictable times. Parents and caregivers sit through a full session with nothing to do. Adult patients arrive early or leave unhurried.

Cuban Coffee Co. brings a warm, low-pressure reason to be there, and a small, friendly counter inside the building that makes the waiting room feel like somewhere good things happen. Neither side has to build anything new. We just point what we already have at each other, on a schedule that works for your practice.

Why it works for you

A real amenity for your families, at no cost or effort on your end.

For Raise The Bar Therapy
What you gain
  • A genuine amenity for parents and caregivers who are already waiting 30 to 60 minutes during a session
  • Zero cost, staffing, inventory, or liability to manage. The cart is a fully separate, insured business
  • A softer, warmer first impression for anxious kids and stressed parents walking in the door
  • Free foot-traffic marketing. Every cart customer sees your sign and your building

How it could actually run

Practical ideas for making the cart feel like part of the practice, set up right in the waiting room instead of outside it.

Schedule

Start with one or two mornings a week, anchored to your highest-traffic appointment blocks. A tight, predictable schedule beats a sprawling one for building repeat habits.

Where it sits

A fixed corner of the waiting room, out of the walkway to session rooms and the front desk. A compact countertop setup, not a full trailer footprint, sized to fit the space you actually have.

Menu built for this crowd

Fast grab-and-go options for parents rushing to drop-off. A steamed-milk "kid drink," no caffeine, so the sibling in the waiting room gets a treat too.

Staff perks

Comped or discounted drinks for RTBT staff, scoped to whichever agreement structure we land on. Small gesture, and usually the fastest way to turn a team into word-of-mouth advocates for the cart.

Loyalty for regulars

A simple punch card for families who come every week.

Trial run first

A four to six week pilot on a set schedule, no long-term commitment. Debrief together afterward and pick the agreement structure that matches what actually happened.

What we would need from you

Short list, nothing that touches your staff's day-to-day.

  • A fixed spot indoorsA corner of the waiting room or lobby, roughly the footprint of a dinner table
  • Signage permissionA small tabletop sign or menu card
  • Open-door accessNon-patients can walk in for a coffee and walk out, without needing to check in as a patient
  • An outlet nearbyStandard power for the espresso equipment, no special electrical work needed
  • A single point of contactFor scheduling, so days can shift around your calendar if needed
  • An insurance exchangeCuban Coffee Co. carries its own general liability and product liability coverage, and can name RTBT as additional insured
Next steps

Let's find the right spot and try it.

  1. Walk the space together to agree on a spot in the waiting room and where signage goes.
  2. Set a trial schedule, for example two mornings a week for six weeks, with no agreement to sign yet.
  3. Debrief after the trial and choose the agreement structure that fits what the traffic actually looked like.
