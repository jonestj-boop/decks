What the audit found

Budget isn't the constraint. The account settings are.

We reviewed $145.5K of spend across 8 campaigns (3 Meta, 5 Google) from May 1 to Sep 21. The same pattern shows up on both platforms: the cheapest traffic in the account (brand search, past purchasers, warm visitors) is being throttled by the settings.

What that means for you: the first fixes don't need more budget. They need the existing budget pointed at the right audiences, with bid targets the account can actually hit.

Spend grew 78% into August. CAC nearly doubled.

Google · What's throttling spend

An 800% ROAS target is hiding River + Sky from people searching its name.

Target ROAS bidding pulls back impressions when a campaign can't hit its target. Google spend barely moved over the summer, but ROAS fell from 9.65× to 3.77× and CAC nearly tripled.

The proposal

How we'd run it.

Meta and Google, managed as one account plan, with separate budgets for new customers, warm visitors and past buyers.

Meta · The plan

Split the account by funnel stage so each audience gets its own budget.

Your retargeting audiences are already segmented well: leads, engagers and past buyers, with clean exclusions. We keep that work and give it room to run.

Prospecting

Net-new customers only

Define an engaged audience at the account level and exclude it, along with purchasers, so cold spend stays cold. That gives you a real new-customer CAC to manage against.

Retargeting

Warm visitors who didn't buy

Move leads and engagers out of the shared CBO campaign into their own budget. Switch the lifecycle setting off "all audiences" and cap bids so the small pools don't throttle.

Retention

The 40% who buy again

Win-back and repeat-purchase get protected budget and their own creative. With a $180–200 AOV and a 40% repeat base, this is the cheapest revenue in the account.

Creative: the add-to-cart drop tells us the ads need to move people to product faster. We'll build product-first and dynamic (DABA) variations for each stage, including AI-produced video, and test them against the current ads.
Google · The plan

Win back your brand searches first, then scale what's working.

Google is under 15% of paid spend today. It should be the cheapest channel you run, because a lot of that demand is people already looking for River + Sky.

Week 1

Reset Brand Search

Bring the ROAS target down to a number the campaign can clear, or move to impression-share bidding, and get back toward the 85% share you had in May.

Weeks 2–4

Fix the product-line PMax

Reset Brooklyn Dress to a realistic target, tighten asset groups and feed titles, and use search-term data to add negatives.

Month 2+

Scale non-brand

Catch-All is healthy. Once brand is covered, we add budget there and test non-brand Search on the product terms where it earns its keep.

Cleanup: two paused campaigns (DemandGen Video and Campaign #1) get retired or rebuilt so the account only carries campaigns with a job to do.
The first 90 days

Quick fixes first, then rebuild, then scale.

Month 1

Stop the leaks

  • Reset Google bid targets, starting with Brand Search
  • Define the Meta engaged audience and exclusions
  • Separate retargeting and retention budgets
  • Connect GA4 + Shopify for one revenue view
Month 2

Rebuild by stage

  • Launch the funnel-stage Meta structure
  • First round of product-first creative tests
  • Tighten PMax asset groups and negatives
  • Report new-customer CAC apart from repeat buyers
Month 3

Scale what's winning

  • Move budget to the stages hitting target CAC
  • Scale Catch-All PMax and test non-brand Search
  • Second creative round built from what won
  • 90-day review and next-quarter plan
How we'll measure it: new-customer CAC, retargeting and retention ROAS reported separately, Brand Search impression share, and landing-page-to-cart rate. All reconciled against Shopify, so we aren't stacking two platforms' self-reported revenue.
