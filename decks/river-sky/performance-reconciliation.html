METHODOLOGY · HOW TO READ EVERY CAC IN THIS DECK
There's more than one way to calculate CAC. Here's what each one means, and why Google's is still an estimate.
Three different formulas appear across this deck. They answer different questions, and they're not interchangeable.
CHANNEL TOTAL CAC
Total ad spend on the channel, divided by total conversions on that channel.
The blunt version. Mixes retargeting and prospecting spend together, and counts repeat buyers alongside new ones.
CHANNEL NEW-CUSTOMER CAC
Spend on the new-audience segment in that channel, divided by new-customer conversions in that same channel.
The sharper version. Isolates new-customer efficiency, but only works if the channel can actually tag which spend and which conversions are new-customer.
BLENDED nCAC (THIS DECK'S ESTIMATE)
Meta's new-audience spend plus all of Google's spend, divided by Shopify's actual new customers.
A workaround, not a clean measurement. Built because Google can't currently isolate new-customer spend or conversions on its own.
WHY META AND GOOGLE AREN'T APPLES TO APPLES
The strictly new-customer version of CAC, attributing spend and conversions to new customers only, is visible on Meta through the prospecting and engaged audience segments used throughout this deck. On Google, that level of tracking effectively stopped after May 5, when the account's campaign types changed, so Google's conversions can no longer be split by new versus existing customer. Google is roughly 20% of total ad spend, this isn't a rounding error, it's a real gap in what the account can currently prove.
WHAT WOULD CLOSE THE GAP
The Blended nCAC used throughout this deck approximates the answer despite that gap, it remains an estimate until new campaigns are set up and more data comes in. The real fix is structural: rebuild the campaigns on both platforms so retargeting spend and new-customer spend are cleanly separated. Once that's done, how much is spent on retargeting, how much on new customers, and the CAC and ROAS for each of those two investments, can be reported directly instead of estimated.

BREAKEVEN nCAC · FIRST ORDER ONLY, NO REPEAT PURCHASES COUNTED
At $190 AOV and 75% product margin, the first-order ceiling is about $140, before shipping and fulfillment.
This is the CAC a new customer needs to clear on their first order alone, without relying on the 40% repeat rate to make them worth acquiring.

$190

PRODUCT MARGIN
75%

BREAKEVEN nCAC CEILING
~$140
First-order net, before shipping and fulfillment costs are deducted. The true breakeven number is lower than this by whatever shipping and fulfillment cost per order.
GAP
Shipping and fulfillment cost per order is the one input we don't have yet, and it's the difference between this being a ceiling and being the exact number. Once that figure is shared, this becomes a precise breakeven CAC instead of an upper bound.

CAC · TWO ESTIMATES, TWO PURPOSES
Two CAC reads: Meta's own new-audience efficiency, and the blended estimate with Google.
Both are estimates, not verified one-to-one new-customer costs. Here's exactly what each one does and doesn't include.
METHODOLOGY NOTE
Meta new-audience CAC (left) uses only Meta's own reported spend and purchases on the prospecting and engaged segments, no Shopify or Google data involved. Blended nCAC (right) divides that same Meta new-audience spend plus 100% of Google's spend by Shopify's own new-customer count, since Google Ads has no way to separate spend between new and existing customers. Engaged is only tracked as its own Meta segment starting September 2026, before that the new-audience figure is effectively prospecting-only. Neither figure is an exact deduplicated CAC.
META NEW-AUDIENCE CAC (EST.), REPORTED BY META
9-month average $91.02, matching the client's own quoted CAC. Uses Meta's own spend and purchase data only, not Shopify.
BLENDED nCAC (EST.), VERIFIED AGAINST SHOPIFY
9-month average $109.74, recent 3-month average $102.13 (-10% vs. first half). Verified against Shopify's own new-customer count.
Data used per month: Meta's prospecting + engaged spend (from Meta Ads) plus 100% of Google's spend (from Google Ads), divided by Shopify's new-customer count for that month (from the Shopify new-vs-returning export). See the next slide for the exact monthly figures behind this.
TREND
Both reads point the same direction, efficiency has genuinely improved since the first half of the year. Meta's own new-audience number lines up with the account's historical ~$90 CAC, and the Shopify-verified blended number is trending toward the $88-100 floor already proven in May and July.

HOW WE GET THERE
  • Reset Search-Brand and Brooklyn Dress PMax ROAS targets to levels the account can actually clear
  • Give the retargeting campaign its own protected budget, not a byproduct of the prospecting CBO
  • Split account structure by funnel stage instead of one broad, undifferentiated pool
  • Keep reconciling every month against Shopify, not platform-claimed numbers
RECOMMENDATION
Branded Search stays its own benchmark, it's neither new nor existing by nature. Everything else on Google gets assessed by audience stage once campaigns are restructured, with likely exceptions for broad PMax placements that can't fully separate signal. None of this requires new spend, it requires giving Google the same audience-stage visibility Meta already has.
