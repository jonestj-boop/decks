What's working

  • Repeat buyers. In a typical month, 60 to 70% of online orders come from returning customers

The math that decides everything

The first bag covers most of what a customer costs. The second bag is the profit.

Working numbers from our call. Gross margin is our 70% assumption until we see your Shopify data, and shipping costs come out of it later.

Average order today
$40
Gross profit on that first order at 70% margin. Spend less than this to win a customer and the first order pays for itself
1.43×
Return on ad spend needed to break even on the first order. $1,000 in ads has to bring back about $1,430 in sales
$73
Most we can pay for a customer and still break even in year one, if 40% of new buyers come back
How we got $73. If 60% of new buyers order once ($57) and 40% become regulars spending about $175 a year, close to the $150 to $200 average you see today, the average new customer brings in about $104 in year one. At 70% margin, that's about $73 of gross profit.
Subscriptions move the line. If a subscribe option lifts repeat buyers to 60%, the same math lets us pay up to about $89 for a customer. Repeat rate matters more to this plan than anything the ads do.

Part two · Keep them coming back

Subscribe and email.

Ads pay for the first bag. Subscriptions and email bring in every bag after it, without paying for the customer again.

Subscribe and save

Start with the buyers you already have.

Most of your online orders already come from repeat customers. Give them a subscribe button and a reason to use it, and some of your revenue becomes predictable before we spend anything on ads.

Weeks 1–2 · Set up

Turn it on

  • Subscription app on Shopify with delivery every 2, 3, or 4 weeks
  • Subscriber discount and perks, set by you (for example 10% off and free shipping)
  • Subscribe option shown first on every coffee page
  • Easy skip, swap, and pause, so people don't cancel
Weeks 2–4 · Existing buyers

Invite your regulars

  • Email everyone who has ordered twice or more
  • Personal note from you on why subscribing helps farmers plan too
  • Follow-up to anyone who reorders by hand
Ongoing · New buyers

Convert first orders

  • Subscribe offer in the post-purchase emails
  • Reminder about 3 weeks after a 12oz order, when the bag runs low
  • Ads retargeting one-time buyers with the subscribe offer
We've done this for a coffee brand. They had a lot of existing customers on subscription. We opened up new audiences on Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok, used the pixel to find buyers like theirs, then ran win-back campaigns asking one-time buyers to subscribe. Subscription revenue grew from $41K to $138K. We'll send you the full write-up.

Bigger orders

Raise the average order without raising prices.

Every dollar added to the average order raises what we can afford to pay for a customer. These are your decisions to make. We'll model each one against your margins first.

Store changes

Make bigger the obvious choice

  • Big-bag price break. Today 24oz costs exactly two 12oz bags. A small discount gives people a reason to size up
  • Free shipping at a threshold just above one bag, so the second bag feels free
  • One listing per coffee with a size choice, so reviews and sales stay together
  • Origin sampler: the four Ethiopian regions in one box, a natural first order and gift
  • Gift subscriptions for the holidays, once subscriptions are live
Products you already sell

Reach buyers others can't

  • Green coffee for home roasters. A small market, but home roasters search for exactly this
  • Seasonal collections you already roast, sent as limited drops to the list
About Amazon: keep it as it is. It sells without ads, and Amazon is a hard place to compete on price. New money goes to your own site, where you keep the customer, the email, and more of the margin.

The day-90 decision

  • Scale if customer cost is under the ceiling and repeat orders are on track. Raise the budget in steps
  • Hold if one is close. Keep the budget, fix the weak spot
  • Stop if neither is close. You keep the subscription setup, the email flows, and a much bigger list
Even a stop leaves something behind. The store changes, the subscription program, and the list keep working for the customers you already have.
