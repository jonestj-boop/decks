Position has degraded every month since May (38.3 → 58.5 in July) while impressions kept climbing — the opposite of a healthy trend, where more visibility usually comes with better rankings, not worse.

What this rules out

This isn't demand drying up. Raw impression volume is at an all-time high. The problem is what's generating those impressions and where it ranks — covered on the next two slides.
Clicks have not kept pace with impressions at any point. Even July's high-water mark (32 clicks on 35,267 impressions) is a 0.09% CTR — far below what a page-one ranking would produce on this volume.
Only 2 days of August data are in yet (GSC's reporting lag holds back the most recent ~3 days) but that's already tracking below July's daily pace on clicks (0.5/day vs. ~1/day) — worth revisiting once the full month closes.

Diagnosis

This isn't the "rankings we earned are sliding" problem. It's dilution.

Putting the last three slides together: the site published a batch of new content, and it's actively pulling attention (and likely internal links/crawl budget) away from the term that used to work.

What happened

A wave of new VoIP/business-phone content went live between the two 30-day windows — 8+ new pages, all ranking position 60–81 (high 60s–80s).

What it's costing

The core term "phone service" fell 86% in impressions and nearly 5 positions in the same stretch — the one query most tied to actual buyer intent.

What's still fine

The homepage held steady, and a couple of adjacent terms ("mobile service," "google voice") actually improved — this is targeted, not a site-wide penalty.
No previous roadmap or audit exists for this account to compare against or confirm intent behind the new content push — this deck establishes the first documented baseline. Recommended next step is a content/internal-linking review of the new pages against the "phone service" money pages, not a fresh strategy rebuild.
Next Steps

Worth a conversation before more ground is lost.

The pages and rankings that earned this account's traffic are still live — this is a maintenance and dilution problem, not a demand problem, which means it's fixable. The longer the new content sits unreviewed, the more it compounds against the terms that matter.
