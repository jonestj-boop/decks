The model · Rent to own

Rent the demand, learn who signs, then own it.

Google and Meta put BridgeVA in front of buyers this month. Content and brand mentions take longer, but the traffic they earn doesn't bill by the click. Rent to own uses the first to aim the second: we only build content around searches and buyers that have already produced signed clients.

Rent · Month 1 onward

Buy the conversations

  • Google Search on hire-intent terms, with job-seeker terms blocked
  • Meta lead ads offering the outsourcing assessment
  • Founder and client video to warm cold audiences
  • Retargeting everyone who visited pricing, compare or a vertical page
→
Learn · Weekly

Follow leads to signed

  • Which searches and audiences book calls that close
  • Which buyer signs fastest: PM, brokerage or investor
  • Which role sells first: ISA, TC, leasing, maintenance
  • Which hook wins: hours saved, cost vs. hiring, or AI+VA
→
Own · Month 1 onward

Build what ranks

  • ClusterMagic articles on the roles and buyers that signed
  • Brand mentions in "best real estate VA companies" lists
  • Cost, comparison and job-description pages that rank
  • As pages rank, bid less on those same searches
Why this fits a placement business: each signed client pays every month for as long as the VA stays. That lifetime value gives paid room to work early. Once we know which searches produce long-term clients, content takes those searches over at a fraction of the cost per client.

Rent · Meta go-to-market

Meta reaches the owner who hasn't searched yet.

Most operators don't Google "virtual assistant" until the pain is acute. Meta reaches them earlier with a useful offer instead of a hard "book a call," and builds the warm audience that converts later.

The offer

Lead with the assessment

  • "See how many hours your team could hand off": the outsourcing assessment you already have
  • The AI+VA guide as a second gated offer
  • Instant lead forms with one qualifying question: doors managed, or agents on the team
  • Book-a-call ads only for warm audiences
The audiences

Owners, not job seekers

  • Broad targeting, steered by creative that names the buyer ("If you manage 200+ doors…")
  • Interest signals around property management software, brokerages and real estate investing, tested against broad
  • HubSpot customer list as a seed and an exclusion
  • Retargeting: site visitors, video viewers, form openers
The creative

Four angles to test

  • Hours back: "Your team gets 20+ hours a week back" (your claim, kept as stated)
  • Cost vs. hiring: a full-time VSM next to a local admin salary
  • Safer than Upwork: trained, managed, replaced free
  • AI+VA: a VA who already works with AI tools
Housing category check: Meta can treat real-estate-related ads as a special ad category, which limits age, ZIP and lookalike targeting. These ads sell a business service, not housing, but we plan audiences and copy that perform under either setting.
Video beats stock: 30 seconds of Amy explaining how matching works, plus two client clips, will outperform polished graphics. Phone-shot is fine. Real faces are the point.

How we set the target: lifetime margin per client × the share you're willing to spend to acquire one = maximum cost per signed client. Divide by the close rate and that's the maximum cost per booked call. Campaigns that can't reach it after a fair test lose budget to the ones that can.
