The search market · Watch out

Half the people searching "real estate virtual assistant" want the job, not the hire.

You see it in your inbox, and Google's suggestions show it too. For the two biggest category terms, most completions are about jobs, salary and training. Run broad ads on those terms without heavy filtering and you pay for job seekers' clicks.

Buyers phrase it differently. They add "for," "company," "services" or a tool name: virtual assistant for property management company, appfolio virtual assistant. Most of the operators you want never type "virtual assistant" at all. They search for the bottleneck.
Search volume has a ceiling. "Property management virtual assistant" runs about 720 searches a month, and most of that isn't buyers. Paid search alone can't fill a 500-placement calendar, so Meta and content have to carry part of the load.

The model · Rent to own

Rent the demand, learn who signs, then own it.

Google and Meta put BridgeVA in front of operators this month. Content and brand mentions take longer, but the traffic and AI recommendations they earn don't bill by the click. Rent to own uses the first to aim the second, so we only build content around the searches and operators that have already signed.

Buy the conversations

  • Google Search on operator-intent and software terms, with job-seeker terms blocked
  • Meta ads reaching operators who never search "VA"
  • Your Apollo list of AppFolio users loaded as an ad audience
  • Retargeting everyone who read an article or visited a niche page
→

Follow leads to signed

  • Which searches and audiences book calls with decision makers

  • Which software angle converts: AppFolio, Yardi, Vendoroo

Why this fits BridgeVA: AI-referred calls already close at close to 100%. Every dollar of paid tells us which problems those operators are trying to solve. Content and mentions then make sure ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity name BridgeVA when they ask.

Rent · Meta go-to-market

Meta reaches the operator who isn't on LinkedIn and hasn't searched yet.

The operators ignoring your DMs still scroll Facebook and Instagram. Meta reaches them with a useful offer instead of a cold pitch, and it's cheap traffic for testing which message lands.

The audiences

Your list, then lookalikes

  • The Apollo/Apify list of AppFolio users you already built, uploaded as a custom audience
  • HubSpot contacts and clients as seed and exclusion lists
  • Interest signals around multifamily, property management software and industry associations, tested against broad
  • Retargeting: site visitors, video viewers, form openers
The offer

Value first, call second

  • The outsourcing assessment: "see what your team could hand off"
  • The property management software guide you already wrote
  • Lead forms that ask for doors managed and role
  • Book-a-call ads only for warm audiences
The creative

Four angles to test

  • Doors without headcount: grow the portfolio, not the payroll
  • Trained on your stack: a VSM who already knows AppFolio and Vendoroo
  • Hours back: 20+ hours a week per hire (your claim, as stated)
  • Track record: the founders who built this twice, explaining why the third time is for operators
Housing category check: Meta can treat real-estate-related ads as a special ad category, which limits age, ZIP and lookalike targeting. These ads sell a business service, not housing, but we plan audiences and copy that perform under either setting.
Real faces beat stock: 30 seconds of you explaining how matching works, plus a clip from the 500-door client, will outperform polished graphics. Phone-shot is fine.

Why Google-heavy: buyer searches are scarce but high intent. $3,000 covers most of the qualified volume on the property management, software and CRE terms. Spending more there mostly buys job-seeker clicks.

Own · ClusterMagic content plan

Answer the operator's real question, then make BridgeVA the obvious next step.

Your library is the right idea. ClusterMagic builds it into connected clusters by operator problem. Articles link to each other and up to the service pages, so Google and AI tools see BridgeVA as the authority on running a multi-door operation.

Cluster 1 · Tools you already run

A VSM inside your software

  • One page per tool: AppFolio, Yardi, Buildium, Vendoroo, RentRedi
  • Screenshots, a short Loom and what a VSM handles in each
  • "Switching software or staffing it?" comparison guides
  • Top property management software, updated yearly
Cluster 2 · Operations by asset type

Scaling the portfolio

  • Multifamily and build-to-rent: leasing and renewals at scale
  • Storage and manufactured housing back-office playbooks
  • HOA and community management workload guides
  • CRE research and asset-management support
Cluster 3 · Org chart & AI

Doors without headcount

  • Redesigning a property management org chart
  • Where AI automation stops and a person has to step in
  • Managed VA vs. in-house hire vs. marketplace
  • Case studies: the 500-door firm and others as they land
Every article sells, quietly: each one carries a lead magnet and links to the matching niche or tool page, so readers move from learning to booking. Articles then go out through LinkedIn, Facebook and BiggerPockets, and can be cut into YouTube videos.
Pick a pace you can hold for 12 months: content compounds. Starting at 25 articles a month and keeping it steady beats 100 a month for a quarter and then stopping.
