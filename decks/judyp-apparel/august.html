*As stated in the May roadmap. Re-pulling April 2026 live today shows 0.42% — a small shift, most likely from Google reprocessing historical data after the fact, not a data error on either side.

Clicks grew far faster than impressions did. A 22% impression increase does not explain a 182% click increase — something about engagement or query mix improved, not just visibility. That's the strongest, cleanest signal in this data.
Average position drifted slightly worse (7.23 → 8.13) even as clicks rose. Not a contradiction on its own — it can happen when a site picks up more long-tail, lower-competition queries. See the next slide for what we could and couldn't confirm about which keywords moved.

What's Driving The Growth

Non-branded search is driving the growth — branded demand actually softened.

Comparing the roadmap's baseline window (Nov 2025–Apr 2026) to the equivalent period since (May 2026–Aug 3, 2026) — two non-overlapping windows of comparable length:

Segment Baseline (roadmap), Nov 2025–Apr 2026 Now, May 2026–Aug 2026 Change
Branded clicks809399-51%
Non-branded clicks11,79519,017+61%
Total clicks12,60419,416+54%
Non-branded search is doing all of the work, and then some. Non-branded clicks grew 61% (+7,222) in the period since the roadmap, while branded clicks actually fell by roughly half (809 → 399). Total clicks are still up 54% only because non-branded growth outweighs the branded decline — people who weren't already looking for JudyP by name are finding the brand through organic search, which is exactly the kind of growth a content or technical improvement produces.
The branded decline is worth a separate look. Fewer people searching "JudyP" by name over a 4-month period could reflect softer brand awareness, seasonality, or a shift in how existing customers return to the site (direct visits, email, saved bookmarks) — GSC alone can't tell us which, since it only sees organic search.
Worth A Second Look

Average position drifted slightly worse — a real signal, even if the cause isn't obvious yet.

The roadmap flagged striking-distance keywords (positions 11–30) as the fastest wins available. We tried to recheck that exact keyword count and hit a data-reliability wall worth explaining before using any number here.

Period Avg. position
April 2026 (roadmap's headline month)7.23
July 2026 (latest full month)8.13
Baseline window (Nov 2025–Apr 2026)6.26
Since-roadmap window (May 2026–Aug 2026)8.17
We deliberately dropped the striking-distance keyword count from this slide. Search Console's per-keyword report only exposes a partial, privacy-filtered sample of queries — in our testing it revealed anywhere from 31% to 55% of actual clicks depending on the period, and the exposed sample isn't stable between pulls. Any specific keyword count we could show you here would be an artifact of that sampling, not a real, comparable number. We'd rather show you one honest signal than a precise-looking wrong one.
Average position, which comes from a complete dataset with no sampling gaps, did drift worse — both in the single-month comparison (7.23 → 8.13) and the multi-month window comparison (6.26 → 8.17). Two explanations fit this equally well: some previously-ranking terms could have broken through to page one (which would actually pull them out of this data and be a good thing), or the account could be picking up a wider spread of harder, more competitive terms as traffic grows. We can't tell which from position data alone.
The direction is consistent across every way we sliced it. Single-month (7.23 → 8.13) and multi-month window (6.26 → 8.17) both show the same modest downward drift in position, with no complete, reliable dataset available to tell us which of the two explanations above is the real driver.

Why we're not calling this a win or a loss

Every engine dropped in cited pages — consistently, not just one outlier. Google AI Mode alone fell from 493 cited pages to 86, an 83% drop, despite being called out in the roadmap as JudyP's strongest AI channel.
A composite score rising while every input metric falls is unusual and doesn't match a typical real-world shift in brand presence over three months. We haven't been able to confirm the cause from SEMrush's public documentation, so we're presenting the numbers as-is rather than interpreting them as a clear win or loss.
