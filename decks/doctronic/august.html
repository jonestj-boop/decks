The Paradox

Three data sources, three different stories.

We pulled Search Console, GA4, and SEMrush AI Visibility data live for this report. They don't agree — and that disagreement is itself the finding.

Search Console

Volatile
Clicks dipped 39% in June, then recovered 43% in July

Rankings and CTR never moved. This reads as normal fluctuation, not decline.

GA4

Growing
Organic sessions hit an all-time high in July (1.18M)

Grew straight through the GSC dip. Worth reconciling — the two sources shouldn't diverge this much.

AI Visibility (SEMrush)

Collapsing
Mentions down 89% May→July, still down ~90% now

The only metric with a clean, sustained, every-engine decline. This is the real signal.

The headline isn't "traffic is down." It's that classic search is holding steady while the site's presence inside AI answers — ChatGPT, Gemini, AI Overview, AI Mode — is quietly disappearing. That's a growing blind spot, not a visible one.

What's behind the session growth

Nearly all of the increase is coming through Google itself — not from other search engines picking up the slack. Google-sourced sessions grew from 478K in May to 1.04M in July. Bing, DuckDuckGo, and Yahoo grew too, but far more slowly, so their combined share of total organic traffic actually shrank over the same period.
But visitors are engaging with the site noticeably less as this volume climbs. The share of sessions that count as genuinely engaged fell from 70% in May to roughly 52–58% since, and average time on site dropped by about a third.
Bottom line: more sessions, but shorter and shallower visits, concentrated almost entirely in one source (Google) and one country (US, up 163% May→July while every other market held flat) — this combination is consistent with lower-quality or automated traffic being counted alongside genuine visitors, not real demand rising. GSC, which only counts actual clicks from real search results, doesn't pick up this kind of traffic — which is likely why it shows the more conservative, June-dip-then-recovery picture instead. This is our best read of the available data, not a confirmed diagnosis; a closer look at GA4's internal-traffic rules and session-level detail would help confirm it.
