The hypothesis

What this test actually answers

The question isn't "can we get clicks" — it's "can we turn a Minneapolis searcher into an active member at a cost that scales?" That's the number that decides whether paid becomes a growth engine or stays a supporting act.

A $10–$20 membership won't pay back a $15–$50 head-term roofing click on day one — no membership does. So this is a learning test, not a profit test: it measures cost per activated member and early repeat-usage signal, and hunts for the cheapest audience and message. We deliberately avoid the priciest head terms and probe the long-tail, comparison and "no-fee" searches the incumbents don't own.

The test design

Two audiences, tightly themed, in one market

Ad set A · primary

Demand — property owners & managers

People searching to hire and to compare bids. Landing page: "Get 3 competitive bids — you pick the pros." This is the audience that generates bid requests, the core value event and the revenue.

Ad set B · secondary

Supply — contractors

Contractors hunting for work without lead fees. Landing page: "$0 lead fees. Lock down your area." Keeps liquidity so demand-side visitors find pros to match — and tests the cheaper side to acquire.

Geo: Minneapolis metro, weighted to the beachhead suburbs — Chaska, Chanhassen, Eden Prairie, Bloomington, Edina, Minnetonka, Maple Grove and out from there. Tracking: GA4 + GTM, conversions defined as a signup and a first bid request — the actions that actually matter.

If it works Go

A segment clears the gates → scale spend on the winners, expand geo to the next metro using the same structure.

If it's close Iterate

Promising but over target → tune keywords, landing pages and offer; re-test the strongest segment before adding budget.

If it doesn't Hold

No segment pays back → we say so, pause paid, and lean on the content engine, which compounds toward near-$0 acquisition.
