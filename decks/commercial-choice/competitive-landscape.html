The players

Three incumbents, one shared playbook

Angi and HomeAdvisor are now the same company (Angi Inc.) running one shared-lead engine; Thumbtack runs a pay-per-contact version of it. All three monetize the contractor — and all three sell the same lead more than once.

Angi
Formerly Angie's List

Directory + shared leads. Contractors buy leads and annual ad plans; the same request goes to several pros at once.

HomeAdvisor
Now part of Angi Inc.

The lead-selling arm — "Angi Leads." Sells each homeowner request to 3–8 contractors who race to respond first.

Thumbtack
Pay-per-contact

No annual fee, but charges per lead on dynamic weekly pricing — and those leads are still shared across pros.

Commercial Choice
The transparent alternative

Flat, low membership. The owner picks up to three verified pros; the contractor pays nothing to receive the bid request.

Follow the money

Why the incumbent model frustrates everyone it touches

The reseller model

One lead, sold to a crowd

  • 1A homeowner submits one request.
  • 2The platform sells it to 3–8 contractors at $15–$100+ each — making money several times on the same lead.
  • 3Contractors race to call first; the homeowner's phone floods with unknown numbers.
  • 4Losers eat the lead cost and bake it into higher prices — which is why so many contractors say "never again."
The Commercial Choice model

One bid, controlled by the owner

  • 1The owner writes an AI-assisted bid request (AliKat).
  • 2They pick up to three verified pros — plus two of their own — and only those get it.
  • 3It drops straight to the contractor's inbox and phone. Both sides know exactly who they're talking to.
  • 4Contractor pays $0 to receive it, so the quote comes back sharper. Commercial Choice steps out of the way.
This is the story to lead with: one bid, the pros the owner chose, and a contractor who pays nothing to quote — so the number comes back honest. Every incumbent weakness (reselling, spam, hidden cost, no control) is a Commercial Choice headline.

The opening

Where Commercial Choice wins

You don't beat Angi by out-spending Angi. You beat them by being the thing they structurally can't be — transparent, member-priced, and controlled by the customer.

Zero cost of acquisition

Contractors pay nothing to receive a bid request — so they can quote their best number instead of padding it to cover lead fees.

No reselling

A bid request goes only to the pros the owner chose — never auctioned to a crowd of strangers.

The owner is in control

They pick who bids, keep favorites honest with competition, and always know who's contacting them.

Flat, honest pricing

$10–$20/yr lifetime membership and a $10 bid fee — no per-lead roulette, no dynamic surprises, no hidden cut of the job.

The incumbents monetize the frustration. Commercial Choice removes it.
That's not a feature list — it's a category position no incumbent can copy without breaking their own business model.
