The good news: Google already tests your pages for hundreds of merchant searches, mostly on pages two and three. You don't need traffic from nothing. You need pages strong enough to move from position 22 to the top 5.

The five moves, in order

  • Let AI tools use your content. One line in robots.txt is telling ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Google's AI features not to use safe.app in answers.
  • Merge the duplicates. 17 pages about reason codes and 11 about "hidden costs" compete with each other. Fold them into a few strong pages.

  • Give buyers a page to land on. Searches like "automated dispute resolution software" already reach page one, but there's no page built to win them.
  • Get named off-site. Brand mentions, reviews and comparison pages are what AI answers pull from.

AI visibility

Your robots.txt asks AI tools to leave Safe out of their answers

Every crawler entry in safe.app/robots.txt, including GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, Claude-Web, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended, carries this line:

Content-Signal: ai-train=no, search=yes, ai-input=no

ai-input=no is a request not to use your pages as source material for AI-generated answers. That's the exact job we want ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews to do with your guides. When we asked ChatGPT for the best chargeback protection app for Shopify on Nov 18, it named Chargeflow, Disputifier and Signifyd. Safe wasn't in the answer.

Fix this first: change it to ai-train=no, search=yes, ai-input=yes. You keep blocking model training and allow AI tools to quote and link you. It's a five-minute change and it unblocks everything else in this section.

Other technical cleanup

  • App pages in Google: app.safe.app URLs with long consent parameters are showing in search. Add noindex to the app subdomain.
  • Two sitemaps: both safe.app and www.safe.app sitemaps are submitted. Keep the www one, since safe.app redirects to it.
  • Dates: posts show the same published and modified date. Update dateModified when a page is actually revised.
  • Disavow: file a domain-level disavow for the link-seller networks behind about 44% of your backlink anchors.

Library structure

  • One hub: "Chargeback reason codes: every code, by network". This becomes the merged destination for the 17 overlapping pages.
  • Network pages: Visa, Mastercard, PayPal, Amex, Discover, Stripe and Shopify. Each has a full code table with plain-English meaning.
  • Code pages for the codes with real search demand, starting with the PayPal codes already on page two. Each one covers what the code means, the deadline, the evidence that wins, and a sample response. Every page links to Disputer.
Why this works for AI too: "What does reason code 83 mean?" is exactly the kind of question people ask ChatGPT. A clean, specific, current page per code is the source those answers cite.
Quality bar: each code page needs its own substance, from the evidence checklist to the deadline to a sample response. Hundreds of thin pages with a swapped number would repeat the duplicate-content problem. We'd publish in waves, ordered by Search Console demand.
