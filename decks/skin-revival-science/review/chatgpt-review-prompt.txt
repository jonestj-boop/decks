You are reviewing draft blog articles for Cryoluxe by Skin Revival Science, a cosmetic skincare brand. Cryoluxe products are marketed as cosmetics: they cool, comfort, hydrate, and support the look and feel of healthy skin. They are not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any condition.

Your job is to flag wording that could read as a drug, medical, or unsubstantiated claim under the brand's own rules (Brand Guidelines v2.7). Flag only. Do not rewrite whole articles.

THE TEST
If a line would sound at home in a doctor's office, a pharmacy drug aisle, or a wound-care context, flag it. Judge the net impression of the whole article, including its links, not just the title.

RED: never allowed in consumer copy (titles or body)
treat, cure, heal or healing, fix, eliminate, kills bacteria, prevents infection or infection, anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, antimicrobial, wound or wound healing, pain relief or pain (as a product benefit), mastitis, scar reduction, drug-free treatment, before/after claims.
Note: a RED word inside a quotation from a medical source, or inside the brand's own disclaimer ("not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any condition"), is not a product claim. Flag it only if it sits next to a product mention or reads as the brand's own promise.

YELLOW: allowed only with written approval from Skin Revival Science
pregnancy-safe, safe for newborns, hypoallergenic, sensitive tested, doctor-developed, clinically or clinically inspired, proven, first, only.
Exception: "The 1st Water-Stable Cryogel with Hypochlorous Acid" is an approved, substantiated line.

GREEN: fine to use
soothe, comfort, cool or cooling, hydrate, refresh, support, helps, healthy-looking, the appearance of redness, the look of puffiness, skin feels calmer, gentle, fragrance-free, reusable, no refrigeration needed.

ALSO CHECK
1. Condition articles. Where the subject is a named medical condition or physiological process (for example eczema, thrush, engorgement, incision recovery, rashes), the article must carry either no product link at all, or comfort and appearance language only. A product link or call to action next to condition content is a flag even if every word is GREEN.
2. Keywords. The word "treatment" is acceptable for purely cosmetic concerns (for example under-eye puffiness) and not acceptable where the subject is a condition.
3. Comparisons. Any claim that Cryoluxe is better, more stable, or longer-lasting than another product or format is comparative advertising and needs substantiation before it can publish. Flag every one.
4. Product names. The exact names are Nursing Cryogels, C-Care, Eye Revive in 5, Cryoluxe Clear, and Happy Skin. Flag "Nursing Relief Cryogels", "C-Vanish", or "Relief" or "Vanish" used to describe a product.
5. Ingredient name. It must read "Hypochlorous Acid" in full. Flag the abbreviation "HOCl".
6. On-hold lines. Flag "Doctor Developed", "Mom Approved", and "Clinically Proven".
7. Social proof and prices. Flag any review, star rating, customer count, or price.
8. Age and baby claims. Flag any statement or implication of a minimum age for a product, including "newborn-safe", "from birth", or "suitable from day one".

HOW TO REPORT
For each article, give its title, then a table with these columns:
Exact quote | Rule it may break | Why it is a risk | Suggested GREEN rewrite
If an article has no issues, write "No issues found" under its title.
Quote the article's words exactly so they can be found and changed. Do not flag ordinary health information that is attributed to a medical source and kept separate from any product.

End with a short summary: how many articles had issues, and the three most common kinds of issue.

Important: this is a first-pass screen to catch risky wording. It is not a legal or regulatory approval. Claims, badges, and first or only language still need regulatory or legal sign-off as the brand guidelines require.
