Who buys and who you're up against

People shop for a will when life changes, not when they get around to it.

Nobody wakes up wanting an estate plan. A baby, a wedding, a house, a diagnosis or a parent's death pushes them to search. Each trigger points to a different plan, a different message and a different channel.

The model · Rent to own

Rent the traffic, learn who buys, then own it.

Paid ads put FeedingBird in front of buyers this month. Content and links take months to rank, but then the traffic is yours and you stop paying for every click. Rent to own uses the first to aim the second, so we only build content around searches and buyers that have already sold plans.

Rent · Month 1 onward

Buy the clicks

  • Google Search on state, price and will-vs-trust searches in live states
  • Meta to life-event audiences: new parents, new homeowners, 55+
  • Retargeting for quiz takers and plan-page visitors
  • Holiday gift card push, then a New Year "get it done" campaign
→
Learn · Weekly

Keep what sells

  • Cost per plan sold by search term, state and audience
  • Will vs. trust mix, and how many will buyers upgrade
  • Which hook wins: price, probate, kids, or "done in an hour"
  • Cut losers fast, move budget to winners
→
Own · Month 1 onward

Build what ranks

  • ClusterMagic articles on the searches and states that sold
  • Brand mentions in "best online will maker" lists
  • Links from financial planners, realtors and family sites
  • As pages rank, bid less on those same searches
Why this order matters for a $99–$399 product: a one-time sale leaves little room for wasted clicks, and the big brands make broad terms expensive. Paid tells us fast which narrow searches and buyers pay back. Content then takes those searches over at a fraction of the cost per sale.

Own · ClusterMagic content plan

Turn the Journal into the place families learn estate planning.

The Journal's voice is right: plain language, a real family story, a clear takeaway. It needs depth and volume. ClusterMagic builds four topic clusters with full-length, attorney-reviewed guides, so Google and AI tools see FeedingBird as an authority and not just a checkout page.

Your state's rules

Cluster 3 · Life events

When life changes

  • Estate planning for blended families

Cluster 4 · The documents

POA, health care, more

  • Financial power of attorney: who and when

Every article sells, quietly: each guide ends with the quiz or the matching plan, and the state articles link to that state's page. Readers decide between will and trust before they ever reach checkout.
Legal accuracy first: drafts cite statutes and court sources and go to Mario or a network attorney for review before they publish. Every piece keeps your "educational content, not legal advice" line.

Get named in the lists buyers and AI tools read.

When someone asks ChatGPT or Google "what's the best online will maker," the answer comes from comparison articles. FeedingBird isn't in those yet. Getting named there, and earning links from the people who advise families, is how a new domain builds trust fast.

Unit economics · Setting the target

Know what a sale is worth before we buy one.

A one-time plan means the first order has to carry most of the ad cost. The table shows the most we could pay per sale and still keep half the revenue. Your real numbers (refund rate, upgrades, add-ons) set the final target at kickoff.

PlanPriceMax cost per sale at 50%
Will, individual$99$50
Will, couple$149$75
Trust, individual$299$150
Trust, couple$399$200

Simple math on list prices, before any promotions. Not a forecast.

Raise the order value, and every channel works harder. Push couples pricing (two plans for $50–$100 more), route quiz takers who need a trust to the Trust Plan, and offer attorney support once it's live. A mix that leans toward trusts roughly doubles what we can pay per sale.
Why trusts lead the paid budget: at $50 per sale, a $99 will is hard to buy profitably on Google, where legal clicks are expensive. Will searches go mostly to content. Paid aims at trust buyers, couples and the gift card window.
Suggested starting ad spend: $3,000–$5,000 a month across Google and Meta for the first 90 days, enough to get clean cost-per-sale data in a few live states. Budget then follows whichever state, plan and audience pays back.
