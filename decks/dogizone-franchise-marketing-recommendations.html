Franchise Marketing Model

Recommendations on the DogiZone Franchise Marketing Structure

A review of the Dog Care Franchise Comparison Matrix, and what we recommend changing before the FDD is finalized.

EMBERTRIBE Prepared for Andrew Fraser · July 2026 · Confidential

Start Here

The Digital Marketing Fee is not what competitors pay their agency

The concern raised on our last call was that our franchise pricing looks significantly higher than the competitive set. That comparison is being drawn against the Digital Marketing Fee row of the matrix, and that row measures something different.

Here is what that fee actually covers across the four brands:

  • Dogtopia: $125/mo for microsite maintenance, local digital ad campaigns, and review monitoring, plus a $700 setup fee.
  • Hounds Town: $125/mo "Local Advertising Management Fee," described in their FDD as in addition to the Local Advertising Requirement.
  • K9 Resorts: $399/mo mandatory SEO package.
  • Camp Bow Wow: no line item, but their required tech stack includes Scorpion, an outside agency.

That $125 to $399 is the franchisor's coordination and technology fee. The actual campaign management is funded out of the Local Advertising Expense line, which runs $2,500 to $5,000 per month across the set. Hounds Town states the mechanism plainly: franchisees "must use designated supplier for social and digital advertising." The designated supplier is paid from the advertising requirement. The $125 is brand overhead.

The recommendation that follows from this: keep your $250/mo digital marketing fee exactly as designed, as DogiZone HQ's technology and brand governance fee. Our management fee should be structured as a carve-out of the Local Advertising Expense, not as a competitor to the $250. Once it is routed correctly, the pricing gap you were concerned about does not exist.

Recommendations

Eight changes we recommend, in priority order

1

Name a designated marketing supplier in the FDD Before FDD locks

This is the highest-leverage item on the list, and Hounds Town gives you the precedent to point to. Mandatory participation across every location is what makes low per-unit pricing possible at all. Without it, every franchisee becomes an individual sale, execution quality varies by location, and brand consistency is unenforceable.

2

Resolve the Local Advertising Expense, which currently exists in two conflicting versions Before FDD locks

The matrix reads "$5,000/mo during the first four months, thereafter 3% of revenue with a $2,000/mo minimum." What you described on our June call was $3,500/mo for year one, then 3% with a $2,500 minimum. These need to reconcile before the FDD locks.

Worth noting: the matrix language is currently word for word identical to the K9 Resorts entry, which suggests it was carried over rather than chosen deliberately.

Our recommendation: $5,000/mo for the first six months, then 3% with a $2,500 minimum. That matches the two most aggressive competitors, who both mandate $5,000/mo early, and it front-loads spend into the window where a new location actually needs it to clear your pre-opening lead requirement. At $3,500 you would sit below both.

3

Standardize the grand opening spend and use it as the lead gate Before FDD locks

This number also appears twice with different values. The executive summary shows $20,000 to $30,000 for pre-opening and grand opening marketing, while the FDD investment table shows Grand Opening Advertising at $10,000 to $15,000.

Camp Bow Wow mandates a $25,000 minimum, verified by the franchisor before opening approval. K9 Resorts requires $30,000. We recommend landing on a $25,000 minimum with franchisor verification. That verification step is also the cleanest way to implement the pre-opening qualified lead requirement you liked from the Camp Bow Wow model, because it is the same gate they already use to enforce it.

4

Reset the $1M in 18 months assumption Follow-up conversation

This is the number that will define whether the marketing program is judged a success, so it is worth grounding in the Item 19 data already in your matrix:

  • Hounds Town, stores open 3 to 5 years: $673K average. Stores open 10+ years: $1.26M.
  • Camp Bow Wow: $993K mean gross, bottom quartile $812K.
  • Dogtopia: $932K average across 191 qualifying outlets.

$1M at 18 months sits above what mature competitors average at three to five years. There is also a direct funding consequence: a realistic year-two location at roughly $650K annually is about $54K per month, so 3% produces only around $1,600. The minimum, not the percentage, is what will actually govern the ad budget for most locations. We recommend modeling the program against the minimum.

5

Add a per-location setup fee Follow-up conversation

DogiZone currently has no setup fee anywhere in the structure. Dogtopia charges $700 plus a separate microsite and social setup fee, and Hounds Town carries one as well.

Every new location requires real launch work: Google Business Profile creation and optimization, a location page, campaign build, and conversion tracking installation. A one-time onboarding fee in the $1,500 to $2,500 range remains competitive against the set and funds the launch build rather than leaving it unfunded.

6

Build escalators into the digital marketing fee Follow-up conversation

Every competitor has one. Dogtopia can move from $125 to $275, Hounds Town increases up to $150 per year, and Camp Bow Wow's brand fund can rise 25% annually. The current "up to $250 per month depending on scope" language is vague in a way that will create friction later. Write it as a defined range with a stated ceiling so it can rise without amending the FDD.

7

Hold the brand fund at 1.5% with a 3% ceiling Follow-up conversation

You are mid-pack here and the structure is sound. Camp Bow Wow's posture of disclosing the fund but not activating it until the system reaches scale is exactly what you described wanting. Keep the 3% ceiling in the document even though you will not use it initially, because this is where national and brand-level marketing eventually gets funded.

8

Confirm the operating model we aligned on Follow-up conversation

To keep per-location pricing low, the program runs as a single ad account with geo-targeted campaigns per location and standardized creative customized only for location name and geography. Reporting runs on a monthly recap rather than standing calls. Google Business Profile is created and optimized once at launch, then handed to the franchisee to manage day to day. Each location receives one page on the existing DogiZone domain. These assumptions are what the pricing depends on, so they should be reflected in the franchise documents.
