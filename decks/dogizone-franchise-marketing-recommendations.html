Start Here

There is no single industry convention for how franchise marketing gets funded

The concern raised on our June call was that our franchise pricing looks significantly higher than the competitive set, based on the Digital Marketing Fee row. Having now worked through the full matrix, the honest answer is that the four brands do not share one model. They split into three approaches, and the row you were comparing against means different things in each.

  • Fee covers some execution. Dogtopia's $125/mo is for "microsite maintenance, local digital ad campaigns" and review monitoring Dogtopia B10/C10. K9 Resorts lists a $399/mo mandatory SEO package Exec. Summary I21. In both, some agency-type work sits inside the monthly fee.
  • Fee is coordination only, execution is funded separately. Hounds Town's $125/mo is "in addition to Local Advertising Requirement" Hounds Town C10, and franchisees "must use designated supplier for social/digital advertising" Hounds Town C9. The supplier is paid from the advertising requirement, not the fee.
  • A hybrid that credits one against the other. Camp Bow Wow charges a $300/mo Marketing Platform Fee that "counts toward Local Advertising Expense after opening" Camp Bow Wow B8/C8. Dogtopia's co-op fee similarly "credits against Local Marketing Commitment" Dogtopia C11.

Worth noting that Camp Bow Wow's $300 is higher than your proposed $250, which undercuts the premise that the set is uniformly cheaper than what you are contemplating.

What we recommend: the Camp Bow Wow hybrid is the cleanest structure and the best precedent for what you are building. Keep your $250/mo digital marketing fee, and write it so that it counts toward the Local Advertising Expense rather than sitting on top of it. That gives you a predictable revenue line at HQ, keeps the franchisee's total marketing cost honest and easy to explain in Item 7, and leaves the advertising requirement as the single pool that funds actual campaign execution. On our June call you had already reached a version of this yourself, when you said the management fee "would need to be within" the local advertising expense. We agree, and the matrix supports it.

Because your percentages carry dollar minimums, and because most locations will sit below the revenue at which those percentages overtake the minimums, the minimum is what a typical franchisee will actually pay. On that basis your draft is at or near the top of the set, not the middle.

Recommendations

Eight recommendations, in priority order

1

Name a designated marketing supplier in the FDD Before FDD locks

Hounds Town requires franchisees to "use designated supplier for social/digital advertising" Hounds Town C9. Mandatory participation is what makes low per-unit pricing possible, because the cost of building campaigns, tracking and reporting is amortized across every location rather than re-sold one at a time. It is also the only reliable way to enforce brand consistency and to get clean cross-location performance data.

Raise this with your franchise counsel specifically. Naming a designated or required supplier carries FDD disclosure obligations, including Item 8 required-purchase disclosure and disclosure of any revenue, rebate or other benefit DogiZone receives from the arrangement. If you intend HQ to retain any margin on the marketing fee, that needs to be disclosed and structured deliberately. We are flagging this because we would be the beneficiary of this recommendation and you should have independent advice on it.

2

Keep your $3,500/mo year-one figure, and resolve which version governs Before FDD locks

Two conflicting versions are live. Your matrix reads "$5,000 per month during 1st four months; thereafter, 3% of revenue (w/ minimum of $2,000/mo.)" Exec. Summary B19. On our June call you described $3,500/mo for year one, then 3% with a $2,500 minimum. These need to reconcile.

Note that the matrix line is word-for-word identical to the K9 Resorts entry Exec. Summary I19, minus K9's grand-opening clause, which suggests it was carried across rather than chosen.

We recommend your $3,500 figure, not the matrix's $5,000. Our earlier verbal steer was toward a higher number and it was wrong. Measured across year one, your $3,500/mo totals $42,000, which is already second-highest in the set, behind only Hounds Town's $45,000 across months 4 to 12 Hounds Town B9 and ahead of Camp Bow Wow's $30,000 Camp Bow Wow B7 and Dogtopia's roughly $24,000 Dogtopia B9. The only true six-month early floor in the data is Dogtopia's "greater of $3,000/month or 2%," which sits below your number. Raising to $5,000 would put you at the top of the market on the single line a prospective franchisee scrutinizes most, and Andres flagged on our call that months one through six are exactly when a new owner is least able to absorb it.

3

Settle on one grand opening number Before FDD locks

This also appears twice with different values. Your executive summary shows $20,000 to $30,000 Exec. Summary B23, while your FDD investment table shows Grand Opening Advertising at $10,000 to $15,000 Single Unit Invest. B13/C13. On our call you said the pre-opening figure was "right around $20,000."

The set runs: Camp Bow Wow $10,435 to $25,000 Exec. Summary C23, Dogtopia $15,000 to $30,000 Exec. Summary E23, Hounds Town a $15,000 required pre-opening package Hounds Town B17, K9 $30,000. Your $20,000 sits comfortably mid-set. We suggest publishing a single figure rather than a range, since ranges in this line invite prospects to assume the top.

One structural point worth copying: Camp Bow Wow specifies that local marketing "cannot be combined with Camp Launch Advertising" Camp Bow Wow C7, keeping launch spend genuinely incremental rather than letting it absorb the ongoing requirement. If you adopt the pre-opening lead requirement you described from Camp Bow Wow's model, that separation is what makes it enforceable.

4

Pressure-test the $1M in 18 months target Follow-up conversation

Worth grounding against the Item 19 data in your own matrix:

  • Hounds Town is the only brand reporting by tenure: stores open 3 to 5 years average $673K, stores open 10+ years average $1.26M Exec. Summary G4.
  • Camp Bow Wow: $993K mean gross, bottom quartile $812K, top quartile $1.3M Exec. Summary C4.
  • Dogtopia: $932K average across 191 qualifying outlets, top quartile $1.3M Exec. Summary E4.

The Camp Bow Wow and Dogtopia figures are system-wide averages across all outlets rather than tenure cohorts, so they are not a direct read on where a location sits at 18 months. Only Hounds Town gives that view, and it points to roughly $673K at three to five years. $1M at 18 months is therefore an ambitious target rather than an impossible one, but we would not build the marketing program's success criteria on it without a second data point.

The funding consequence matters more than the target itself. At $650K annual revenue, 3% produces about $1,625, so your $2,500 minimum governs. For most locations, most of the time, the minimum is the budget. We recommend modeling the program against the minimum and treating the percentage as upside.

5

Add a per-location setup fee of $700 to $1,000 Follow-up conversation

DogiZone currently has no setup fee. The benchmark is Dogtopia's $700 one-time Microsite & Social Media Setup Fee, due when the microsite launches Dogtopia B19/C19, and Hounds Town's $300, due at lease signing Hounds Town B19/C19.

Each new location needs Google Business Profile creation and optimization, a location page, campaign build and conversion tracking install. A one-time fee of $700 to $1,000 sits at the top of the observed range while remaining defensible against it, and funds the launch build rather than leaving it unfunded. We had originally suggested $1,500 to $2,500; that was above market and we have corrected it.

6

Write an explicit escalator into the digital marketing fee Follow-up conversation

Every comparable has one. Dogtopia's $125 "may increase to $275" Dogtopia B10, Hounds Town's "may increase by up to $150/year" Hounds Town C10, and Camp Bow Wow's $300 Marketing Platform Fee "may increase up to 25% annually" Camp Bow Wow C8. Your current "up to $250/month depending on scope" Exec. Summary B21 is vague in a way that will create friction at renewal. Define the starting figure and the ceiling so it can move without amending the FDD.

7

Confirm whether the brand fund is 1% or 1.5% Follow-up conversation

This is a third place where your documents disagree. Your matrix shows 1.5% with a 3% ceiling Exec. Summary B18; on our June call you said 1%, not established initially. Since you flagged that the DogiZone column is not fully current, we assume 1% is the live figure, but it should be settled deliberately.

Either number is competitive: Camp Bow Wow is at 1% Camp Bow Wow C6, Dogtopia and Hounds Town at 2% Exec. Summary E18/G18. One correction to note, since it affects how you frame this to prospects: Camp Bow Wow's 1% is active and collected twice monthly, not merely disclosed. What they have not established is the Regional Advertising Co-op Camp Bow Wow C10. If you intend to disclose the fund now but not collect until you have a group of locations, that is a defensible position, but it is not the Camp Bow Wow posture. Keep the 3% ceiling in the document regardless, since this is where brand-level marketing is eventually funded.

8

Reflect the operating model in the franchise documents Follow-up conversation

The pricing depends on these assumptions, so they should be written down: a single ad account with geo-targeted campaigns per location and standardized creative varying only by location name and geography; monthly recap reporting rather than standing calls; Google Business Profile created and optimized once at launch then handed to the franchisee for day-to-day management; and one location page per franchise on the existing DogiZone domain. Most of these we discussed in June. The single-ad-account structure we proposed but did not explicitly confirm with you, so please treat it as an open item.
