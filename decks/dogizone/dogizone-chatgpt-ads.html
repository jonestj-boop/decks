What Changed

ChatGPT Ads Just Got More Capable — and More Eligible

Ads had been quietly live since a Feb 9, 2026 pilot. Self-serve rolled out in stages — CPC bidding and a lowered spend minimum on April 10, that minimum dropped to $0 on May 5, then broadened publicly on July 22 under the "Advertise in ChatGPT" name. Two of those updates matter most for DogiZone: standard ads no longer require a product catalog, and campaigns can now optimize toward the same conversion events — calls and lead forms — DogiZone's paid program already runs on.

What's New

  • → The standard ad format — title, copy, image, landing page — is open to any eligible advertiser. Product feeds are a separate, optional bulk-creation tool for large catalogs, not a requirement
  • → New oCPC (Conversion-optimized) campaigns: pick one conversion event — lead submission, phone call, or similar — and delivery optimizes toward it
  • → "Local services" is one of a short list of explicitly allowed advertiser categories during this early phase

What to Know Before Testing

  • → Ads shown only to ChatGPT Free and Go plan users — Plus, Team, and Enterprise stay ad-free
  • → Conversion tracking runs through OpenAI's own Conversions API / JS pixel — CallRail shipped a direct integration in June 2026, but there's no confirmed native integration yet for Nimbata, DogiZone's call-tracking platform
  • → Eligible categories are explicitly framed as "early phase" — the list could tighten or shift as the program matures
  • → Platform is genuinely new — no multi-year benchmark history like Google or Meta

How It Works

Two Ways In — Not Just One

Product feed ads are real, but they're an optional bulk-creation tool for catalog businesses — not a gate on who can advertise. Here are both paths, and where each maps onto DogiZone.

📞

Standard Ads + oCPC

  • Build one ad — title, copy, image, landing page — same as any Search or Display ad, no catalog required
  • Set the campaign objective to Conversions (oCPC), pick one event: lead submission or phone call
  • Fits DogiZone's service business — Boarding, Training, Daycare, Grooming
📦

Product Feed Ads

  • Connect a Merchant Center-style catalog (100+ SKUs)
  • ChatGPT auto-generates sponsored cards directly from product data
  • Optional bulk tool — fits DogiMarket's retail catalog specifically
🎯

Context-Matched Serving (Both Paths)

  • Advertiser supplies "context hints" — topics, scenarios, intent
  • OpenAI matches those to live conversation intent via auction
  • Ads labeled "Sponsored," shown below the response — never inside it
Why this matters for DogiZone: the service business doesn't need a product feed to test this channel. A standard ad optimized toward phone-call or lead-submission conversions is the more direct fit — and it's the same conversion logic already running in DogiZone's Google Ads account today.

Where This Fits

Both Sides of the Business Have a Path In

DogiZone isn't limited to a retail-only test. Services and retail each have a route onto this channel — they just look different, and the real risk isn't eligibility.

🐕

Services — ~88% of Revenue

  • Lodging/Boarding, Training, Daycare, Grooming — plus smaller Activities & Transportation lines
  • Converts via phone call — "local services" is an explicitly allowed ChatGPT ad category
  • Path in: standard ad + oCPC, optimized toward phone-call or lead-submission conversions
  • Real constraint: needs OpenAI's Conversions API/pixel wired up first — no native call-tracking integration confirmed yet
🛒

DogiMarket Retail — ~12% of Revenue

  • Dog food, accessories, gear sold through dogizone.com
  • Merchant Center feed already exists — 8 past Shopping campaigns, all currently paused, $0 spend
  • Path in: Product Feed Ads, once the feed is audited and reactivated
  • Real constraint: catalog has been dormant — product data needs a health check before spending against it
📋

The Honest Read

  • Eligibility isn't the blocker for either side — "local services" is allowed, and DogiMarket already has a feed
  • The real open question is attribution maturity: conversion tracking here is newer than Google/Meta's, and phone-call attribution specifically needs proper setup to trust
  • Right move: pilot both tracks small and isolated — don't let one block the other

Risks & Guardrails

What Could Go Wrong — and How We'd Catch It

The blocker here was never eligibility. It's attribution maturity — and that's a real, current limitation worth naming plainly.

Real Risks

  • → Conversion tracking runs through OpenAI's own Conversions API/pixel — no confirmed native integration with Nimbata (DogiZone's call-tracking platform) yet, so early phone-call attribution may be more directional than definitive
  • → The retail feed has been paused across all 8 campaigns — product data may be stale, mispriced, or out of stock after being dormant
  • → Eligible categories are explicitly framed as "early phase" by OpenAI — the list could tighten before either pilot has time to prove out
  • → Platform is genuinely new — no multi-year benchmark data for either the local-service or pet-retail category

Guardrails Built Into This Plan

  • → Verify Conversions API/pixel accuracy before either pilot spends seriously — start Services on the lead-submission event, which is easier to confirm than phone-call
  • → No retail ad spend until the feed audit confirms the catalog is healthy enough to test
  • → Both test budgets capped and fully isolated from the $10.2K/mo existing Google program, and from each other
  • → Fixed 30–45 day checkpoint per track, with Services and Retail reviewed and decided independently
