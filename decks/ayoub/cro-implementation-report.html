Mobile speed scores, before and after

Google scores page speed from 0 to 100. Under 50 counts as poor, 50 to 89 needs work, and 90+ is good. In June all three pages scored under 50 on mobile. Our developer swapped your caching setup for LiteSpeed Cache, cleaned up unused Elementor code across the site, and tuned the three pages your visitors land on most.

"Main content loads" is Google's Largest Contentful Paint. "Page freeze" is Total Blocking Time, how long the page ignores taps while it loads. June figures come from the CRO audit. November figures are from Google PageSpeed Insights on Nov 18, 2026. Scores move a few points between runs.

Why the freeze time matters: a phone visitor who taps "Call" or "Get a Quote" while the page is frozen gets no response. On the contact page that delay was over a full second. It's now zero on all three pages.

Measuring results

How we’ll measure the impact on leads

We won’t promise a number. We’ll compare real lead data from before and after launch. Most changes went live in October, so the fair test is at least two months of post-launch data against the same stretch before it, adjusted for seasonal swings and any changes in ad spend.

What we’ll compare

  • Contact form submissions against visits to the contact page.
  • Phone calls from the site.
  • Online bookings through Schedule Online, now tracked in GA4 (C-01). Since this tracking is new, bookings will be measured going forward rather than against a before number.
  • Where each lead came from, once the hidden lead-source fields are live (H-07).
One gap to close first: lead source isn’t being recorded yet. That’s in progress (H-07), and we want it live before we run the comparison so you see the full picture.
