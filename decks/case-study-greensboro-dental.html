The starting point

Strong reputation, little search visibility

  • A 4.9-star practice that new patients rarely found unless they already knew the name
  • Key service pages hidden from Google, with slow load times on mobile
  • No clear link between ad spend and the patients who actually showed up and paid
  • The doctor wanted higher-value cases, especially implants, over call volume
What we did

Google Ads

  • Five campaigns covering preventive, restorative, implants, emergencies and general services, run Monday to Thursday on a $4,000 monthly plan
  • Rebalanced budget mid-month and shifted spend toward implants

Local SEO

  • Rebuilt the Google Business Profile with services, products, photos and scheduled posts
  • Published 10 town pages, rewrote 19 service pages, and fixed hidden pages and slow load times

Reporting tied to revenue

  • Matched ad costs to front desk data on new patients, no-shows and treatment value

What we learned

More calls only matter if patients show up

Front desk data showed that missed appointments, not lead volume, were the biggest drag on cost per new patient. We gave the front desk a follow-up script and a simple tracking log, so missed appointments get a call back instead of being lost.

What's next

Automation in the practice's own software

The next phase sets up reminders, missed-appointment recovery, review requests and treatment follow-up inside the practice's own management software. The goal is to keep more of the patients the ads already bring in.

The takeaway: paid search fills the schedule now, local search builds visibility the practice doesn't pay for per click, and front desk data shows which of those calls become paying patients.
