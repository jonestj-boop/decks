Overview

What this service is

Reviews are the biggest thing we can change that affects where a local business ranks on the map. We build the system that asks every customer for a review, run it every month, and reply to every review so the client doesn't have to.

What the client gets

  • Google Business Profile tune-up and a direct review link
  • Automated review requests by text and email after each appointment, set up in their existing patient or customer messaging software
  • A check that the client's existing QR cards and signs point straight to the Google review link
  • Scripts and a 30-minute training for the doctor, hygienists and front desk
  • A one-time campaign asking past customers for reviews
  • Replies to every Google review within 48 hours, written to be HIPAA-safe
  • A monthly report: new reviews, rating, progress against target, competitor review counts
The rule everything else depends on: we ask every customer the same way and never offer anything in exchange for a review. A fast way to lose a client's profile is to filter out unhappy customers or pay for reviews. Google removes those reviews, and it can suspend the profile.
Compliance rules

Non-negotiables

These come from Google's review policies, the FTC's rule against fake reviews and review suppression (2024), and HIPAA for healthcare clients. Read them before any client kickoff. If a client asks us to break one, escalate to the account lead. Don't negotiate it yourself.

✕

No incentives to customers. No discounts, gift cards, raffle entries, free whitening or donations "per review". This applies even if the reward is offered for honest reviews.

✕

No review gating. Don't ask "how did we do?" first and send only happy customers to Google. Every customer gets the same Google link, and we never leave out customers we think are unhappy.

✕

No staff, family or owner reviews. Not about the practice and not about competitors.

✕

No writing reviews for customers and no scripted review text. We can suggest what to mention ("what you came in for"). We never supply the words.

✕

No review stations in the office. No office tablets, front-desk computers or "use our Wi-Fi to leave a review". Customers use their own phones. Reviews posted from the business's own network often get filtered.

✕

No PHI in replies (healthcare). Never confirm someone is a patient and never mention a treatment, visit date or condition, even if the reviewer brought it up first.

✕

No texts without consent. Only text customers who agreed to text messages at intake. Respect STOP and opt-outs right away.

✓

Allowed: rewarding staff for asking (leaderboards, team lunches for hitting targets), asking every customer in person, sending reminders, and reporting reviews that genuinely break Google policy.

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–2

Setup

1

Kickoff and intake

  • Get Manager access to the Google Business Profile (never take Primary Owner)
  • Identify their customer messaging software (Weave, RevenueWell, NexHealth, Solutionreach, Lighthouse 360, Birdeye, etc.) and get admin access
  • Confirm that text consent is collected at intake, and get a written OK to send review texts
  • Get monthly customer volume (unique visits a month). This sets the target.
  • Name the client champion and the escalation contact for negative reviews (the owner or doctor)
  • Record the baseline: review count, average rating, reviews added in the last 90 days, reply rate
  • Record 3–5 map competitors' review counts and ratings (whoever shows in the top 3 for their main search)
2

Business Profile tune-up

  • Primary and secondary categories match how customers search (e.g. Dentist, Cosmetic dentist)
  • Services list is complete. Hours and holiday hours are correct. Booking link works.
  • Name, address and phone match the website exactly. No keywords stuffed into the business name.
  • At least 15 real photos: team, office, exterior, signage. Add new ones monthly.
  • Description is written and website links use UTM tags
  • Reply to every existing unanswered review using the templates below
3

Review link and QR check

  • Copy the direct review link from the Business Profile ("Ask for reviews" / "Get more reviews")
  • Test it on iPhone and Android, signed in and signed out
  • Scan the client's existing QR cards and signs. They must go straight to the Google review link, with no in-between "rate us" page, because that counts as gating.
  • If a QR code goes to the wrong place, send the client the correct link to update it. We don't design or print materials.
  • Confirm cards are at checkout and signs are in treatment rooms
4

Automated review requests

  • First text goes out 1–2 hours after the appointment is marked complete
  • One reminder 72 hours later, only if they haven't clicked. Never more than 2 messages per visit.
  • Each customer is asked at most once every 6 months, even if they visit more often
  • Email as a backup for customers without text consent
  • Messages go to every completed appointment. Don't filter by provider, procedure or satisfaction score.
  • For minors, the request goes to the parent or guardian on file
  • If the client has no software that can do this, recommend our approved tool (priced separately)
  • Test it end to end on a staff phone before going live
5

Team training (30 minutes)

  • Explain why reviews matter: they're how new customers find the practice on the map
  • Go through the in-person scripts below and role-play the ask in the chair and at checkout
  • Go through the compliance rules, especially no incentives, no office devices and no PHI
  • Agree the team goal and staff reward (the client decides the reward)
  • Set up the weekly count for the champion: a whiteboard or a Slack/text update
Phase 2 · Weeks 3–6

Launch and past-customer campaign

This is where the first big jump comes from. Customers from the last 12–18 months who already like the practice and have never been asked usually bring in the biggest single month of reviews.

  • List: the client exports every customer seen in the last 12–18 months. Take out only people who opted out of texts or emails, have no valid contact details, or have died. Don't take anyone out for being unhappy, because that's gating.
  • Remove anyone who has already left a Google review, where you can match them by name
  • Pace it: split the list into 4 weekly batches. A steady stream of reviews looks natural to Google, while a huge one-day spike can get reviews filtered.
  • Channels: text to customers who consented, email to everyone else, and one reminder per channel
  • Watch: check new reviews daily during the campaign and reply within 24 hours
Pacing check: if a single day brings in more than about 3 times the client's normal weekly reviews, pause the next batch for a week. Reviews that Google filters don't come back.

Negative review protocol

  • Within 24 hours, post the short holding reply below. It takes the conversation offline and gives no details.
  • Send the review to the client's escalation contact, who calls the customer if they can identify them
  • Never argue in public, never mention care or billing details, and never say whether the reviewer was a patient
  • If the review breaks Google policy (spam, off-topic, conflict of interest, hate speech), report it through the Business Profile and log it. Don't promise the client it will be removed.
  • Never ask a customer to change or remove a review in exchange for anything

Asking for reviews

Change the practice name and contact name. Keep the asks short and the same for every customer. Never include suggested review wording.

Checkout · front desk"You're all set! One small favor: we're trying to reach more people in [City]. If you scan this card, it goes straight to our Google reviews. It takes about a minute."

Text reminder · 72 hrs (once, only if not clicked)Hi [First name], just a quick reminder from [Practice]. If you have a minute, we'd love your Google review: [review link]. Thank you!

Reply templates

Replying to reviews (HIPAA-safe)

Change the wording every time so replies don't read as copy-paste. Use the reviewer's first name only if it shows on the review. Never mention a treatment, visit or result, even when the reviewer did.

5 starsThank you so much, [First name]! We really appreciate you taking the time to share this. Our team works hard to make every visit comfortable, and we're glad that came through.
5 stars · variationThis made our day, thank you! It means a lot to our whole team. We're grateful for kind words like these.
3–4 starsThank you for the feedback, [First name]. We're always working to improve, and we'd welcome the chance to hear more. Please call our office manager at [phone] whenever it's convenient.
1–2 stars · holding reply (within 24 hrs)We're sorry to hear this and take all feedback seriously. Because of privacy rules we can't discuss details here, but we'd like to talk directly. Please contact our office manager, [Name], at [phone].
Unclear or suspicious reviewThank you for your feedback. We take every concern seriously and would like to understand more. Please contact our office at [phone] so we can follow up directly.
Why no details: under HIPAA, confirming someone is a patient counts as disclosing health information, even when they've identified themselves publicly. That's why every reply stays general.
