Why volume is flat

Paid traffic follows spend, and spend went down

  • Every source of volume is paid. Meta (1,068) and Google (442) account for 61% of all sessions. Paid sessions grow when budget grows, not because time passes.
  • Daily spend fell about 20% from roughly $206/day in August to roughly $164/day in September (Scott's numbers, which Camila confirmed). Flat-to-lower spend producing flat sessions is the expected result, not a failure in itself.
  • Optimizing for Purchase instead of Add to Cart (early Sept) tells Meta to find fewer, likelier buyers, so it trades reach for intent. Orders rose from 2 to about 5 a week after the switch.
  • Pausing Google Search took out roughly 50–70 sessions a week. The week of Sept 21 dropped to 11 Google sessions, which is most of the dip Scott is reacting to.
  • Organic isn't contributing yet. See the SEO section. There's no compounding layer under the paid traffic.
Don't lead with this. It's true, but it sounds like an excuse if it comes first. Camila's "sessions are a tricky metric" reply already landed badly: Scott pushed back the same day. Agree with him first, then explain.
What we own

Four problems we own

01

Meta traffic bounces 87% of the time, with an average visit of 23 seconds

Across 1,068 Meta sessions, 44 added to cart (4.1%) and 13 purchased. Meta still produces most of the sales, but a large share of its clicks are worthless. In the week of Sept 14, 213 Meta sessions averaged 2.95 seconds with a 93% bounce rate. One UTM line (facebook / paid / facebook, 179 sessions) averaged 2.85 seconds. That pattern fits Audience Network or accidental in-feed taps.

Fix this weekPull the placement breakdown · exclude Audience Network · watch landing-page-view rate, not clicks
02

Google Search: 442 sessions, 3 add-to-carts, 0 purchases

The pause was the right call, and Scott pushed for it. Shopify can't separate paid from organic Google because gclid traffic carries no UTMs, but blog landings are close to zero, so nearly all of this was paid. PMax is the live test now. Its first three days look more engaged than Meta, but it has no purchases yet.

HoldReport PMax against the gate list Scott asked for
03

About a quarter of "store sessions" aren't customers at all

588 sessions (24%) came from outside the US and Canada, mostly Bangladesh, Pakistan, India, the UK, South Africa and Nigeria, with zero purchases. 414 of them are "Direct" with a 157-second average, which looks like developers, freelancers or vendors working on the site rather than shoppers (a Fiverr referrer shows up too). Another ~120 sessions came from affiliate-network referrers (impact, fatcoupon, linkbux, awin) with no orders. All of this inflates the dashboard number Scott watches and drags conversion rate down.

Fix in reportingReport qualified sessions (US/CA, human, non-bounce) as the headline traffic metric
04

We launched without Search Console, and Google never found the blog

Our roadmap said "Connect GA4 + GSC at launch." Search Console was only verified on Oct 1 (domain property, owner access@embertribe.com), it has no performance data yet, and no sitemap has been submitted. The URL Inspection API shows the homepage is indexed (last crawled Sept 15), but the /blogs/journal index and every article we tested come back as "URL is unknown to Google." GA4 is still not visible to us. This is our miss, and the fix is quick.

Fix todaySubmit sitemap.xml · request indexing for the 40 live articles · get GA4 viewer access
SEO yield

40 articles live, and Google doesn't know they exist

The first 40 articles have been published since about Aug 3. Only 12 sessions have landed on any /blogs/ page since then, with no add-to-carts. The reason isn't article quality or a slow ramp. Google has never discovered the blog. Results from URL Inspection (Search Console API, Oct 1):

The pages themselves are indexable: no noindex, robots.txt allows /blogs/, and Shopify's sitemap lists all 40 articles. The problem is that nothing told Google about them. No sitemap was submitted, Search Console wasn't set up, and there are no backlinks pointing at the articles yet.

  • Own this before Scott finds it. He's paying for SEO as acquisition and wants a 90-day yield read. Right now the honest read is "zero, because the articles were never indexed." Better that he hears it from us, along with the fix.
  • Fix it today: submit /sitemap.xml in Search Console, request indexing for the 40 live articles, and check for discovery within 7–14 days. The 90-day clock effectively starts at indexing, not at publish.
  • Reset the projection honestly. The roadmap's ramp (about 6,900 organic visits a month by month 12) assumed indexing from launch. Restate it from an October start.
  • Hold the next 40 drafts until the first 40 are confirmed indexed, or publish them alongside the sitemap fix. Either way, don't promise October traffic from them.
How to frame the call

Agree, redefine, commit

1

Agree with him, plainly

"You're right. Sessions have been flat since mid-August, and a lot of what we've been buying isn't qualified. Here's the breakdown." Don't defend the metric.

2

Redefine "qualified traffic" with him, then baseline it

Propose: human sessions from the US/Canada that don't bounce. Baseline is about 30–48 a week since paid started. The last three days hit 50. This is the number to track week over week, and it maps to the "qualified sessions" line in his October list.

3

Show that the funnel works once traffic is qualified

About 6% of qualified sessions purchase. The lever is buying fewer junk clicks, not changing the site. That fits his agreement to freeze site changes.

4

Commit to specific fixes with dates

  • Meta placement audit and Audience Network exclusion this week, with cost per qualified session reported weekly
  • Separate Your Baby Club traffic with UTMs before it starts, so "paid vs YBC" is measurable from day one
  • Sitemap submitted and articles queued for indexing today, plus GA4 access. SEO goes on the MER page with indexed-page count, impressions and rankings, not only sessions
  • Weekly pack (Mon–Sun) leads with qualified sessions, cost per qualified session, purchases, CPA and MER
  • Ask whether the large discounts are comps or samples: $1,927 gross vs $1,158 net, and the week of Sept 28 shows $509 gross vs $49 net. Those orders shouldn't count toward CAC
His real deadline is Oct 31. He wants to raise spend but won't fund a 0.34x ROAS. Present qualified sessions and cost per qualified session as the evidence that will justify scaling, not as a new way to grade us more kindly.
Method & caveats
  • All figures come from Shopify Analytics (ShopifyQL, sessions table, human-only filter), pulled Oct 1, 2026. Weeks run Monday to Sunday. Data runs through Sept 30.
  • "Qualified" = US/CA sessions × (1 − bounce rate) per week, rounded. Shopify doesn't expose engaged sessions directly.
  • Meta = referrer facebook/instagram or utm_source facebook/paid. It includes about 30 organic social sessions.
  • Google can't be split into paid and organic in Shopify, because ad clicks carry gclid, not UTMs. Search Console was verified Oct 1 and has no performance data yet. Index status comes from the URL Inspection API.
  • Shopify shows 23 orders and 21 completed-checkout sessions. Scott cited 16 through Sept 24 from the ad reports. The difference is the Sept 25–30 orders plus any non-session orders (draft/manual).
