Bailey's CBD:
the implementation scope behind the content plan

We delivered a CRO audit and a three-month SEO roadmap. The client's own brief is bottom-of-funnel conversion. This note lays out what has to change on the site for that content to pay off, what is genuinely left to sell once the audit is in the client's hands, and what to say in the conversation.

The situation

Aaron wants conversion. The content will land on a site that leaks.

Aaron's direction, from Slack: commercial, long-tail, bottom-of-funnel content with no condition claims. The roadmap honors that. Month 1 is 45 product-and-label articles, 15 of them rewrites of the money pages, and every one of them links to a product page. So the content program's results depend on the product page, the cart and the checkout doing their job.

The pitch in one line: the roadmap moves the blog from the wrong readers to buyers, and the audit tells the client how to catch them. Those two together are the sale. Implementation is a line inside it, not a second deal.

Two facts shape the sale. First, the client makes site changes with Claude. Between our walkthroughs on October 14 and 15 the theme shipped a cart drawer with a free-shipping bar and a product row, which closed one of our findings before we sent it, and Aaron will almost certainly run the audit through his own agent. Second, the client already wrote an FDA/FTC content guide and asked us to follow it. Together they describe a client who can execute a backlog fast and needs someone to decide what goes into it, check what comes out, and keep it compliant. That is the scope. Build hours are optional.

The drawer is the case in point. It shipped in a day. Its cross-sell is the catalog in store order with "Select Options" pickers instead of one-click adds, the cart page did not get the free-shipping bar, and the drawer now puts health-outcome testimonials ("Charlie's seizures have reduced dramatically") in front of every buyer, which the client's own guide rules out. Fast shipping without a review step is how a fix becomes a new finding.
What the client has from us

Two deliverables, already QA'd and live

  • CRO audit (client.embertribe.com/baileys-cbd/cro-audit.html): 28 issues across performance, UX, visual messaging, checkout, urgency and social proof, each with a specific Shopify, app or theme fix, sorted into three implementation phases. Re-verified against the live site on October 15.
  • SEO roadmap (growth-roadmap.html plus the keyword workbook): 150 articles over three months, 75 rewrites and 75 new, every row graded against the client's FDA/FTC guide, with 53 redirects, 39 slug renames and a legal hold on the horse content.
  • A Month 1 intake email (drafted, not yet sent): the product fact sheets, lot reports and reviewer names we need before writing starts.

Everything below is work that neither deliverable includes. The audit tells the client what to fix. The roadmap tells them what to write. Nobody is yet scoped to do the fixing.

What is left to sell

The audit is the suggestion. This is what is left.

The audit already tells Aaron what to change and where. His agent can execute most of it without us, and it should; that is the deliverable he paid for. The question for sales is whether Aaron wants someone accountable for the result, or just the list. The options below are ordered by how defensible they are. Hour figures are scoping estimates, not quotes.

1

Funnel review and measurement

CoreEst. 6 to 10 h a month

The piece the client cannot get from his agent. Written into the content retainer, not sold as a second deal.

  • GA4 fixed first: internal and developer filters, Digioh events off, search tracking repaired, so the baseline is real (one-time, 4 to 6 h)
  • A definition of done for each audit item the client takes on: what changes, where, and what must be true afterwards
  • A check of each release on the live site: does it do what the finding asked, did anything get left behind, does it meet the client's own compliance guide
  • A monthly funnel note against the October baseline: add-to-cart, checkout and payment-step rates, blog-to-product clicks, revenue by landing group

Why it holds up: nothing in the client's setup confirms that a change moved the funnel, and GA4 is miscounting today (455k empty search events, protection add-on inflating add-to-cart, unfiltered internal traffic). An agent closes tickets. This closes outcomes.

2

The content plan's site dependencies, as dated milestones

Inside the content engagementBlog template 8 to 16 h, redirects 6 to 10 h

Not a CRO sale. These are things our own program needs done, so they belong in the content engagement with dates, whoever does the typing.

3

Settings sprint

OpenerEst. 8 to 12 hours, no developer

The audit's Phase 1: ten changes in Shopify and app settings. These live in the Digioh, ShipInsure and payment-gateway dashboards rather than in theme code, so they are the items least likely to be in the agent's backlog already, and the fastest way to get our hands on the store.

  • Popup off on cart, checkout and after add-to-cart; once per 7 days
  • Protection unchecked by default in the drawer and the cart
  • Every wallet the CBD-approved gateway allows; pickup points off; app banner off on mobile
  • Countdown tied to the BESTLIFE coupon in words, or removed; subscription led with "save up to 30%"
  • Guarantee line under Add to Cart; Dr. Silver named once documented

How to position: the first two weeks of the engagement, or goodwill inside the retainer. Do not give it away without item 1 attached.

Three scenarios to put on the table

  • Each 0.1 point of site conversion rate on the current 77,295 sessions is about 77 orders, or about $4,700 a quarter at the current order value. The audit's checkout fixes target a step where 43% of checkout sessions currently fall out.
  • Recovering one in ten of the abandoned add-to-cart sessions is about 126 orders, or about $7,600 a quarter. That is the number the popup, protection add-on and cart fixes are aimed at.
  • Every 1,000 blog sessions that move to product-facts pages converting at the product-page landing rate (3.95%) is about 40 orders, against 0.6 orders today. That is the roadmap's job, and it only holds if the product page converts.
Do not say: a percentage lift, a payback period or a benchmark. The audit deliberately gives none, because CBD stores cannot use Shopify Payments, Shop Pay or PayPal, and the gateway limits what the checkout can offer. Stay on the client's own numbers.
Risks and limits

What to know before the call

  • The client's compliance guide governs everything. Several site elements the audit touches (the serving chart, the badges, the testimonials, the research tile) need the client's compliance owner before they change. We spec and build; we do not approve claims.
  • The site changes without notice. One finding closed itself in a day. Any scope should be re-verified against the live site in the week it is signed.
  • The gateway decides the wallets. Express checkout is Google Pay only and some of that is outside anyone's control. Do not promise Apple Pay or Shop Pay.
  • The horse line is on legal hold. 11 planned keywords and all 66 live horse URLs wait on the client's legal reviewer. Keep it out of any scope.
  • The client builds with Claude. Expect every finding to be checked by Aaron's agent against the live site before the call; the audit was re-verified on October 15 for exactly that reason, and the three findings the drawer closed were retired or rescoped. Expect some fixes to be in his backlog already, so lead with sequencing, review and measurement rather than with the list.
  • An agent closes tickets, not outcomes. Nothing in the client's setup confirms that a change moved add-to-cart, checkout or payment rates. That measurement loop is the part they do not have and we do.
  • Pricing is not in this note. The hour ranges are scoping estimates from the audit work. The standalone implementation number is small by design; the size of this account is the content retainer.
The conversation

Suggested shape for the client call

1

Open with the dependency, not the audit

"The content plan you asked for is built. Three of its preconditions are site changes, and the first one is due before the first condition article publishes." That frames implementation as part of the thing Aaron already wants.

2

Show the leak on their numbers

Add to cart to order, the 43% checkout drop, the blog at 0.06%. Three numbers, all theirs. Then the three Critical findings and the fact that one High finding was already fixed by their developer this week.

3

Offer the review layer, and expect "my agent has this"

Ask what his agent has already built or queued from the audit. When he says his agent has it, agree, then ask who checks it and who measures it. If there is no answer, that is the scope: item 1 inside the content retainer, the dependencies as dated milestones, the settings sprint as the opener. Build hours only if he asks.

4

Questions sales needs answered

  • What has Aaron's agent already changed or queued from the audit, and who reviews what it ships before it goes live?
  • Who owns GA4, and can fixing the tracking be the first measurement step?
  • Who is the compliance owner? Nothing on the product template moves without them.
  • Can we get collaborator access to Shopify, Digioh, ShipInsure and the payment gateway for the settings sprint?
  • Is the Month 1 intake email going out with the roadmap, so the fact sheets arrive before writing starts?
