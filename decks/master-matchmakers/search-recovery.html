Finding one · highest priority

The sitemap lists none of the 205 articles, and nothing points Google to it

The site does publish an XML sitemap, at /sitemap.aspx. It contains 75 URLs and every one of them is a main site page. Not a single blog article appears in it, including all 80 we have delivered this year. Separately, the line in robots.txt that would tell Google where the sitemap lives is commented out and still carries the placeholder domain it shipped with.

What robots.txt says today
#Sitemap: http://www.DomainNamehere.com/sitemap.aspx

User-agent: *
Allow: /tv-media/blogs/id/     # Force Google to allow the blog ID parameters
The comment marker at the start of that first line means no search engine is told the sitemap exists. The domain in it was never filled in.
Why this one matters most
  • Articles are found only by following links, so new posts are discovered slowly and unevenly.
  • Google has no last-modified signal for any article, so refreshed posts are not prioritised for recrawl.
  • It affects every article already published and every one still to come.
  • The fix is a settings change in the blog module plus one uncommented line. No content work.
Finding two

Every page on the site links to eight addresses that redirect

The navigation and footer templates still point at the pre-migration URLs. Because they sit in the template, every one of the 507 live pages carries the same eight redirecting links, which is 4,393 internal links in total. Each is a single clean hop rather than a chain, so nothing is broken. It is waste rather than damage, and it is one template edit.

Every internal link is a vote for the page it points at. Sending all of them through a redirect means the site is voting for addresses that no longer exist, and it is one reason the old capitalised URLs took so long to disappear from Google's index.
Also worth knowing. We checked whether any of these were chains of two or more hops, which is where real loss happens. None are. Each resolves in a single step.
Finding three

41 pages carry no canonical tag, including the pages that sell

A canonical tag is the line that tells Google which address is the real one for a page. Where the site has them they are correct, and all 404 blog URLs carry one. The gap is on the main site pages and on five location pages, which are exactly the pages that pick up traffic from multiple address forms.

Texas and Virginia are the live example. The site's older capitalised addresses have almost all been consolidated by Google since June, down from 53 of them drawing impressions in May to two in the last week. Those two are /Texas-Matchmaker and /Virginia-Matchmaker, and both of the pages they point at are on the missing canonical list. The redirects are doing their job. The canonical tag is the second signal that finishes it.
Where canonicals exist, they are right. The blog module serves each article at several address forms and every single one points back to the correct current URL. 404 of 404 checked. That part of the site needs nothing.
Finding four

75 referring domains are delivered to the generic About Us page

Other websites have linked to Master Matchmakers city pages for years using addresses like /AboutUs/NewYork. Every one of those redirects to /about-us rather than to the city page it names. A redirect onto an unrelated page is commonly treated as a dead end, so that authority is not reaching the market it was aimed at.

Findings five to seven

Three smaller items, all cheap to close

None of these are urgent on their own. They are listed because they are quick, and because two of them are actively creating pages nobody wants in Google's index.

Five
The sign-up form is generating hundreds of indexable pages
Every page links to /register, and the link carries a different return address each time, so each one is a separate URL. They have no noindex tag and no canonical. We stopped counting at 223 variants, and five are already picking up impressions in Search Console. The login page next to it is correctly set to noindex, so this looks like an oversight rather than a decision.
Six
Two retired posts are held in the index by a temporary redirect
When a post is taken down the platform issues a 302, which tells Google the removal is temporary and to keep the old address indexed. Both posts we asked to be removed sit in that state. A 301 to the replacement, or a 410, ends it properly. Worth checking whether the platform allows the choice.
Seven
The non-www address takes a detour through insecure HTTP
https://mastermatchmakers.com is the second most linked address on the whole site, with 231 referring domains. It redirects to the http version of the www address, and only then to https. Two hops, through an insecure one in the middle. It should go straight to the secure www address.
Also noted and worth a pass when someone is already in the templates: nine pages carry two H1 headings where there should be one, and 19 images across the site have no alt text, ten of them on the photo gallery page.
