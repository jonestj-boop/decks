AUDIENCE SEGMENTS · WHERE THIS FITS THE STRATEGY

Four segment-level calls worth making before the next budget cycle.

Keep retargeting lean and catalog-led (~10–12% of budget)The warm pool is small and already seeing ads ~3.8 times per person (slide 11), so it can’t absorb much more spend. Run it as catalog retargeting, not website-audience ads (0.43×).
Broad / Advantage+ stays the growth engineA $401 cost per purchase is the price of prospecting. Judge it against the ROAS target agreed with DEVATA from its goals and margins (slide 15), not against 1.0×.
Retire the interest stack as separate ad sets191 ad sets returned 0.70×. Interests and lookalikes become audience suggestions inside Advantage+, not ad sets of their own.
Use first-party lists as signals, not segmentsCustomer and ATC/IC lists feed Advantage+ as signals and power the existing-customer budget cap and new-vs-existing reporting.
Retargeting cost per purchase
$192
DPA / catalog retargeting, 10% of spend, the only segment above 1.0× ROAS.
Prospecting cost per purchase
$401
Broad / Advantage+, 44% of spend, the account’s new-customer engine.
RECOMMENDATIONThis isn’t “retargeting good, prospecting bad.” It’s fewer, bigger ad sets: a thin catalog retargeting layer, Broad / Advantage+ as the engine (Meta’s own recommendations point the same way, slide 16), and audience knowledge fed in as signals instead of 191 separate micro-tests.

Best Sellers list visible on site

The site hides sold-out items, so it shows only 18 of the 40 products on DEVATA’s internal Best Sellers list. 22 are hidden; 18 of those are fully sold out.
In-stock products with gaps
76 of 179
Have at least one material or size variation sold out (179 = 284 − 105). Many available products run on low inventory across variations.
Proof, channel and feed gaps
No reviews on siteNo ratings on product pages even though the Trustpilot app is installed. Trustpilot shows ~4.5–4.6 on ~92 reviews per search snippets (unverified).
Macy’s may capture Meta-driven demandMarketplace listing seen at $315 vs $360 on devata.com (search snippet, unverified); those sales are invisible to the pixel. Some of DEVATA’s own ad copy also says “Also available on Nordstrom, Macy’s.” in headlines and primary text, which sends Meta-driven buyers off-site.
Feed quality weakens catalog adsGold and silver share titles (e.g. “Clover Bracelet” ×14; 191 unique titles across 284 products), products carry no tags, and transparent PNG main images can render on black.
What this means for catalog ads
Product sets should exclude out-of-stock items, and the catalog should sync availability so ads never send a buyer to a sold-out page or size.

We recommend adding a “Notify me when available” button, so a sold-out visit becomes a captured lead instead of a lost sale.
Strengths to keep
Free US shipping, no minimum · 30-day returns · gift wrap · Affirm 6-mo 0% · Shop Pay / Afterpay · 10% email signup
Method and disclaimer: product availability was pulled from the Shopify store’s public JavaScript/JSON product data and spot-checked on individual product pages to confirm it. It may differ from DEVATA’s actual inventory; in some cases “sold out” may reflect only a few size or material variations being available.
FIX FIRSTFix stock visibility in catalog ads and add proof before adding budget. Otherwise extra Meta spend keeps landing on sold-out variations and review-free product pages, which is why LPV → add-to-cart hasn’t moved (slide 4).

ACCOUNT ARCHITECTURE · TESTING ROADMAP

Account architecture is the key to success here. These are the experiments we’d run, in order, to learn how to get the most out of the account.

PriorityExperimentWhat it isWhat it teaches us
START WITH1 · Combined catalog + conversion campaignOne Advantage+ Sales campaign mixing catalog ads and purpose-built creativePools every purchase into one learning signal; the base every other test is measured against.
START WITH2 · One campaign, each creative a different productAds built around concepts: gifting, 14K “forever” pieces, motif edits like Clover / Love Knot, price-band gift guidesShows which products and concepts pull buyers in, inside the same campaign.
NEXT3 · Separate by ticket valuePrice tiers as creative angles and catalog product sets (“gifts under $150”, “14K core $280–500”, “fine $500+”)Builds on the AOV shift toward 14K gold (slide 5) and shows how each price tier responds.
NEXT4 · Separate by audience: new / engaged / existingA thin catalog retargeting layer plus an existing-customer capShows what each stage adds, with frequency kept in check (slide 11).
LATER5 · Separate by categoryRings, bracelets, earrings and necklaces as their own groupsOnce volume grows, shows whether a category earns a budget of its own.
APPROACHEach experiment runs long enough to read before the next one starts, and whatever wins is folded into the core structure.
