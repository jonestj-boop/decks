Why this plan

Why this plan and not a bigger one

The selection is the work you are paying for. Here is what we kept, what we cut, and why.

The pillars

High-Risk Pregnancy Conditions · 34 items

Anchored to your thirteen consultation pages. Your placental complications page already ranks 1.8 and your cervical insufficiency page 5.0, so the cluster has proven ground to build on. We take the specific and clinical terms here. Head terms like gestational diabetes sit at difficulty 96 and belong to national publishers.

Prenatal Testing and Fetal Diagnostics · 13 items

Anchored to your seven MFM test pages and your ultrasound service. Chorionic villus sampling ranks 2.0 and your biophysical profile page ranks 4.0 while earning 18 clicks from 45,220 impressions. Most of this pillar is rewriting what already ranks.

Working With a High-Risk Specialist in Los Angeles · 14 items

Anchored to your second-opinion page, your at-home care page and your concierge positioning. This pillar is a hypothesis we want to validate with you on the call. You told us you want to compete with traditional obstetricians, and nothing on your site currently offers routine care, so we built the argument from what your site can prove: 24/7 access, telemedicine, home and travel visits, board certification, and privileges at six named hospitals. Whether you want to take low-risk patients as their primary obstetrician is the first question on our list.

Procedures and Birth Planning · 6 items

Anchored to cerclage, gentle C-section, natural birth and D&C. Your cerclage page is the single largest rewrite opportunity on the site. The D&C cluster is real volume at low difficulty and you already have the page for it.

Twin and Multiple Pregnancy · 5 items

The strongest cluster in the research. We take the clinical half only: di/di, mono/di and monochorionic diamniotic, average difficulty 33.2. Someone searching those words was told at a scan what she is carrying. We leave the conception-odds half alone. Those searchers are curious, and curiosity does not book a consultation.

What we deliberately excluded

  • Why this plan is 72 pieces and not a round 120. We build these to 120 and this one came in at 72. The reason is the niche, and it is worth knowing before you spend anywhere. Maternal-fetal medicine is deep but narrow: your whole field is about thirty-five distinct clinical topics. After filtering the research to what you treat and to searchers who could become your patient, 1,177 keywords were still on the table that this plan does not already use, and 1,149 of them were different ways of typing a question it already covers. 'What is preeclampsia', 'signs of preeclampsia' and 'preeclampsia symptoms' are one page to Google. Writing them as three would split your own authority three ways. So the plan is 21 new articles, and the other 51 pieces are work on pages you already have, which in your case is where the return is anyway.
  • The 4 link passes, and why we are not trying to rank those posts. Four rows in the plan are marked Link pass. They cover your biggest posts, including the ureaplasma piece that takes 2,394 clicks a quarter on its own. We are deliberately not trying to rank them higher, because that audience is national and cannot become your patient. We are also not deleting them. The work is internal links and a relevant next step, so the fraction of those readers who are local and pregnant reach a service page instead of leaving. It is the only traffic in this plan you have already paid for.
  • Why most of the new articles are not local. Fair question, given everything on slide 2. Two reasons, both from your own data. First, the local demand is genuinely small: across 90 days the 549 geo and near-me searches you appear for drew 7,782 impressions and 50 clicks between them. There is no honest way to build forty local articles against that. Second, local intent does not land on articles, it lands on your service pages, and this plan rewrites 19 of them and builds out 10 more. The new articles exist to make those pages rank, by building the depth around each condition that search engines and AI assistants read as expertise. The difference from what you have today is that every one of them points at a page that can book a consultation, instead of standing alone.
