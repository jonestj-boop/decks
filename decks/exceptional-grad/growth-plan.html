Beyond the fixes · Brand mentions

Get named when applicants ask AI who to hire

When someone asks ChatGPT for the best med school admissions consultant, the engines build their answer from third-party roundups and reviews. Right now those articles name your competitors.

In your July audit, AI engines quoted exceptionalgrad.com pages six times but named ExceptionalGrad only twice, an AI visibility score of 14, in the band SEMrush calls invisible. Your content program fixes the citation side: it makes your site the place engines pull facts from. It cannot put your name in other people's "best consultants" articles, and that is where recommendations come from.

This is also the honest answer to the rankings problem from July. You can't outrank U.S. News for "best admissions consultants," but you can be named in the roundups the engines already trust. That's what a brand mention is: an editorial placement, a listicle, review or buyer's guide on an established site (DR 30 to 60, at least 1,000 monthly visitors), written for a query your applicants actually run. Topics are agreed with you up front; after that it adds nothing to your review queue.

  • First 30 days: topics agreed, first placements live, and a baseline of mentions by engine plus a set of applicant-style prompts we run through ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode

Nobody can promise that a specific engine names you for a specific query, and you should be skeptical of anyone who says they can. What we commit to is the placements: how many, the site quality, and the topics.
