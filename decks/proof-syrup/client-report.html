How the funnel was meant to work

The content is the top of a two-stage funnel

Content like this isn't built to sell on the first visit. Its job is to win cheap, brand-new reach through search, then hand those visitors to Meta retargeting, which converts them once they know you. The blog earns the audience; the ads close it. Worth being clear on who owns which stage: the content and SEO is our work; the Meta retargeting that converts it runs through your paid-media team. The two have to connect for either to pay off.

Where the return got stuck

The two halves of the funnel haven't been connected

This is the most common failure mode when content and paid media sit with different teams: the audience gets built, the retargeting that converts it is run separately, and the two never line up. In the account, it shows up three ways.

  • 77% of Meta budget went to cold prospecting at 1.37x, while the more efficient 2.2x retargeting got 23%.
  • The 60,866-reader content audience was never retargeted as its own segment. Retargeting mostly hit product-page visitors and past customers instead.
  • The dedicated retargeting is paused right now, even though your audience is the largest it has ever been.
