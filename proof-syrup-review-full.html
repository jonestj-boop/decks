Part 1

The dispute

What the client raised — and how we checked it

Cloud Earl made three claims. We pulled GA4 and the Meta ad export to test each honestly.

"Everything we spent failed epically to produce anything resembling a profit on the SEO work."
"All of the organic traffic converts … about 90% less than other traffic."
"Massive amounts of errors and incorrect links scattered through nearly all of it."

Read this before the numbers

  • Revenue tracking started March 2025. Before that GA4 logged sessions but $0 revenue — ecommerce wasn't configured. Nothing before Mar 2025 can be judged for ROI, either way.
  • Attribution is last-click unless a section says first-touch.
  • The "errors/broken links" claim is not a GA4 question — it needs a crawl or Search Console coverage data, and GSC isn't currently shared with us. Flagged in Part 6.

Part 3

The strategy behind it

Content was Stage 1 of a two-stage funnel

The plan was a standard DTC funnel: win net-new, top-of-funnel reach with organic content at low, compounding cost — then retarget those visitors on Meta to convert them. In that model the blog's job is audience, not checkout. Judging it on last-click blog sales measures the top of the funnel against the bottom's job.

Part 5

The execution gap

The proven-efficient leg was starved and is now switched off
  • 77% of budget went to cold prospecting at 1.37× ROAS, while the 2.2× retargeting leg got just 23%.
  • The blog pool was never the retargeting target at scale. Only ~$18k (12%) hit warm site-visitor audiences — mostly product/DPA viewers — and $15k went to existing customers (retention, not blog-sourced).
  • Retargeting is off right now. The RT ad sets (MOF, REPEAT) started Jul 2025 and read "not delivering"; DPA and MEGA Retargeting are inactive — even as the pool sits at its largest.

The profit lives in retargeting (2.2×) — the smallest, now-dormant slice. The client's "no profit" instinct isn't baseless at the blended level; the point is the profitable stage got defunded.
Part 6

Verdict & plan

Where this actually nets out
His claim gets right
  • SEO content produced no meaningful direct revenue ($864 / 0.03%).
  • "Converts ~90% less" is directionally true — the blog is ~99% below commercial organic.
  • Blended paid economics are thin after COGS.
Needs nuance
  • Content did build 68k sessions / 60,866 net-new users of real visibility.
  • Retargeting works (2.2× ROAS) and GA4 undercounts Meta 2.5×.
  • Commercial organic is healthy (3.22% CVR, ~$100k) — the #2 revenue channel.
Can't confirm yet
  • The "errors/broken links" claim — needs a crawl or GSC coverage.
  • Pre-Mar-2025 ROI — revenue wasn't tracked.
  • Exact blog-assisted revenue — needs BigQuery / GA4 Explore.

The recommendation

  • Rebalance budget toward retargeting — it delivers 32% of purchases on 23% of spend at 2.2×. Shift dollars out of 1.37× cold prospecting.
  • Build blog / site-visitor custom audiences and turn retargeting back on — 60,866 readers sit un-harvested.
  • Run both stages in phase for 60–90 days with a holdout, and report on margin-ROAS, not revenue.
  • Get Search Console access (or run a crawl) to verify or dismiss the errors/broken-links claim, then fix whatever's real.
The one handoff already working: organic-introduced buyers closed $10,226 via email (133 orders). The pool converts when it's worked — email is quietly doing at small scale what funded Meta retargeting is built to do at large scale.
Suggested posture with the client: own the direct-revenue shortfall honestly, show that the profitable stage of their own funnel was the one that got defunded, and reframe the ask as "let's rebalance into retargeting, harvest the audience the content built, and measure end-to-end on margin" — not "trust that it worked."
