The case for the funnel: TOF content → Meta retargeting

The content investment was Stage 1 of a two-stage funnel. The client's own Meta ad data now proves the model works — and shows the funnel was under-executed, not disproven.

The strategy, restated

Content was never meant to convert on click one

The plan was a standard, sound DTC funnel: use organic content to win net-new, top-of-funnel reach at low, compounding cost — then retarget those visitors with Meta ads to convert them once they're in the pool. In that model the blog's job is audience, not checkout. Meta closes.

Judging Stage 1 by last-click blog purchases is a category error — it measures the top of the funnel against the bottom of the funnel's job. That reframe is the spine of this whole dispute.

Stage 2 — the honest gap

The proven-efficient leg was starved and is now switched off

A credible case names its weak points. The Meta data exposes three, and they're all about execution, not the idea:

  • 77% of budget went to cold prospecting at 1.37× ROAS, while the 2.2× retargeting leg got just 23%. The money was concentrated in the least efficient stage.
  • The blog pool was never the retargeting target at scale. Only ~$18k (12%) hit warm site-visitor audiences — much of it product/DPA viewers — and $15k went to existing customers (retention, not blog-sourced net-new). The specific blog→retarget loop was never funded.
  • Retargeting is off right now. The dedicated RT ad sets (MOF, REPEAT) started Jul 2025 and read "not delivering"; DPA and MEGA Retargeting are inactive — even as the blog pool sits at its largest.

Profitability caveat, stated plainly: even on Meta's generous attribution, blended 1.56× revenue-ROAS on a ~$70 AOV syrup is likely underwater after COGS and shipping. The profit lives in retargeting (2.2×) — the smallest, now-dormant slice. So the client's "no profit" instinct isn't baseless at the blended level; the point is that the profitable stage was the one that got defunded.

The fix — and how to prove it forward

Complete the loop the data says is efficient

The strategy doesn't need a new thesis; it needs to be run as designed. Every lever points the same way:

  • Rebalance budget toward retargeting. It already delivers 32% of purchases on 23% of spend at 2.2× ROAS. Shift dollars out of 1.37× cold prospecting into the warm leg.
  • Build blog / site-visitor custom audiences and turn retargeting back on. 60,866 net-new readers are sitting un-harvested. Point warm ads at them specifically — not just product-viewers and existing customers.
  • Run both stages in phase — sustained content + funded retargeting against that pool — for 60–90 days.
  • Measure with a geo or audience holdout so the incremental read is clean and neither side has to argue attribution again.
  • Report on margin-ROAS, not revenue-ROAS. Bring COGS in so "profit" is measured on contribution, not top-line.
The one handoff already working: organic-introduced buyers closed $10,226 via email (133 orders). The pool converts when it's worked — email is quietly doing at small scale what funded Meta retargeting is built to do at large scale.

The argument, in one paragraph

How to make the case without overreaching
The content investment did its job as the top of a funnel — 60,866 net-new, low-cost, compounding visitors — and grading that on last-click blog sales is a category error. The client's own Meta data proves the second stage's mechanism: retargeting converts at 2.2× ROAS and $31 per purchase, versus 1.37× and $51 for cold prospecting, and GA4 was undercounting Meta by 2.5×, so the "it didn't convert" verdict is partly a measurement artifact. What went wrong is execution, not strategy: 77% of budget went to cold prospecting, the blog pool was never retargeted at scale, and the profitable retargeting leg is now switched off. The honest ask isn't "trust that it worked" — it's "the efficient half of this funnel was defunded; let's rebalance into it, retarget the audience the content built, and measure on margin with a holdout."

This concedes the thin blended economics, reframes the KPI correctly, and grounds every claim in the client's own GA4 and Meta data — a position that strengthens under scrutiny rather than collapsing.
